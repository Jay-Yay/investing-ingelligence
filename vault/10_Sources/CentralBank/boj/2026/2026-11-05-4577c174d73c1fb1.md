I. Summary of Staff Reports on Economic and Financial Developments5 
A. Market Operations in the Intermeeting Period 
The Bank had been conducting money market operations in accordance with the 
guideline for money market operations decided at the previous meeting on July 30 and 31, 
2026.6  The uncollateralized overnight call rate had been in the range of 0.976 to 0.980 
percent. 
Meanwhile, the Bank had  conducted Japanese government bond (JGB) purchases 
of about 2.5 trillion yen per month in accordance with the plan for the outright purchases of 
JGBs decided at the June 2026 meeting. 
 
B. Recent Developments in Financial Markets 
In the money market, the uncollateralized overnight call rate had been at around 1.0 
percent. The general collateral (GC) repo rate had been at around the same level as the 
uncollateralized overnight call rate. As for interest rates on term instruments, yields on three-
month treasury discount bills (T-Bills) had increased. 
The Tokyo Stock Price Index (TOPIX) had risen, reflecting factors such as favorable 
corporate results in Japan and the United States. Yields on 10 -year JGBs had increased, 
moving in line with U.S. interest rates, while market participants had raised their expectations 
for the terminal policy rate. The liquidity indicators in the JGB markets continued to improve. 
In the foreign exchange market, the yen had appreciated against both the U.S. dollar and the 
euro, mainly on the back of coordinated foreign exchan ge intervention by Japan and the 
United States and market views on Japan's monetary policy. 
 
C. Overseas Economic and Financial Developments 
Overseas economies had grown moderately on the whole, although some weakness 
had been seen in part, partly due to the impact of the situation in the Middle East. The U.S. 
economy maintained solid growth on the whole, although some weakness had been seen in 
part. European economies remained resilient on the whole, but downward pressure on 
domestic demand had been observed, reflecting the impact of the situation in the Middle East.

The Chinese economy had picked up on the whole, mainly supported by an increase in exports, 
although consumption had lacked momentum. Growth in emerging and commodity-exporting 
economies other than China had improved moderately on the whole, although some weakness 
had been seen in part. 
As for the outlook, overseas economies were expected to come under downward 
pressure for the time being due to the impact of the situation in the Middle East. Thereafter, 
however, based on the assumption that the impact of the situation in the Middle East would 
ease, overseas economies were projected to continue growing moderately, partly supported 
by global AI-related demand. Regarding the outlook, attention was warranted for the time 
being on how the future course of the situation in the Middle East would  affect global 
financial markets and the global economy; in addition, there remained high uncertainties, for 
example, over developments in global AI-related demand. 
With respect to overseas financial markets, although high uncertainties over the 
situation in the Middle East remained, market sentiment continued to be at an improved level, 
as robust corporate profits had been maintained. Long-term interest rates in the United States 
and Europe had risen significantly, as attention continued to be drawn to inflationary pressure 
stemming from factors such as prolonged tension over the situation in the Middle East. U.S. 
stock prices had risen, reflecting favorable corporate results. European stock prices were more 
or less unchanged over the intermeeting period; they had been weighed down by higher crude 
oil prices, although solid corporate results had been viewed positively by market participants. 
Meanwhile, currencies in em erging economies were more or less unchanged over the 
intermeeting period, albeit with fluctuations due to factors such as a change in market views 
on U.S. monetary policy. Crude oil prices had risen, with continued tension over the situation 
in the Middle East. 
 
D. Economic and Financial Developments in Japan 
1. Economic developments 
Japan's economy had recovered moderately, although some weakness had been seen 
in part, partly due to the impact of the situation in the Middle East. Regarding the outlook, 
the economy was likely to decelerate somewhat for a time. This was because, although solid 
global AI-related demand and the government's various measures were likely to provide

5 
support, the economy was expected to be pushed down by a deterioration in the terms of trade 
reflecting the rise in energy and raw material prices. 
Exports and industrial production had started to increase, albeit moderately, against 
the backdrop of factors such as the solid increase in global AI-related demand. Regarding the 
outlook, exports and industrial production were expected to continue increasing moderately, 
mainly led by  capital goods and IT -related goods, on the back of solid global AI -related 
demand. 
Corporate profits had been at high levels, against the backdrop of factors such as the 
solid increase in global AI-related demand, while they had been  affected by the situation in 
the Middle East. Under these circumstances, business fixed investment had been on a 
moderate increasing trend, despite being affected by a rise in construction costs. Regarding 
the outlook, business fixed investment was likely to follow an upward trend, underpinned by 
factors such as moves to clear order backlogs for existing investment projects, in addition to 
investment in AI -related areas for capacity expansion. That said, for the time being, the 
growth momentum in business fixed investment was likely to remain subdued , mainly for 
construction investment, due to factors such as the impact of the rise in construction costs and 
delays in construction projects. 
Private consumption had been resilient against the background of an improvement 
in the employment and income situation, although weakness had been seen in household 
sentiment. The consumption activity index (CAI; real, travel balance adjusted) had increased 
for the April-June quarter of 2026, and had declined slightly for July, relative to that quarter. 
Based on anecdotal information from firms, statistics published by industry organizations, 
and high-frequency indicators, private consumption for August seemed to have declined from 
the previous month, due in part to weather conditions, including torrential rain and heavy rain, 
and with the leveling off of a temporary increase in demand for durable goods, such as 
automobiles, stemming from institutional facto rs. Consumer sentiment deteriorated sharply 
in March and April, reflecting the unstable situation in the Middle East and a rise in inflation 
expectations, but continued to pick up thereafter. Regarding the outlook, while price rises 
were expected to weigh on real disposable income, private consumption was highly likely to 
remain resilient for the time being, pushed up by the government's various support measures 
and a front-loading of demand for goods such as air conditioners, in addition to the wealth 
effects from higher stock prices.

6 
Labor market conditions remained tight. Under these circumstances, the 
employment and income situation had improved moderately. The number of employed 
persons had increased at a pace of around 0.5 percent on a year-on-year basis, but the growth 
momentum had recently  slowed, reflecting the decline in the number of self -employed 
workers and family workers , which tends to fluctuate significantly . Nominal wages per 
employee continued to increase steadily, albeit with fluctuations. With regard to the outlook, 
employee income was likely to continue increasing steadily, albeit with fluctuations, with 
corporate profits remaining on an uptrend. 
As for prices, in international commodity markets, crude oil prices had seen a rapid 
and significant rise due to the military conflict in the Middle East starting at the end of 
February. Subsequently, these prices had declined temporarily, reflecting the signing in mid-
June of the memorandum of understanding aimed at bringing the conflict in the Middle East 
to an end, but had recently seen a clear upturn, due to the resurgence of the military conflict. 
Copper prices remained elevated. Meanwhile, market pric es of food had  risen slightly. The 
year-on-year rate of increase in the producer price index (PPI) had been in the range of 7 -8 
percent, pushed up by a significant increase in prices of petroleum and coal products, and of 
chemicals and related products -- reflecting the impact of the situation in the Middle East -- 
as well as by a rise in prices of nonferrous metals and machinery driven b y the expansion in 
global AI-related demand. With moves to pass on the rise in personnel expenses and other 
factors to selling prices continuing, the year-on-year rate of increase in the services producer 
price index (SPPI, excluding international transportation) had been at around 3 percent. 
Despite factors such as the effects of the government's measures to reduce the household 
burden of higher energy prices, the year-on-year rate of increase in the consumer price index 
(CPI, all items less fresh food) had been moderately rising recently, being in the range of 1.5-
2.0 percent, as the effects of a rise in import prices ha d been emerging, with moves to pass 
on wage increases to selling prices continuing. Inflation expectations had risen moderately. 
With regard to the outlook, the year -on-year rate of increase in the CPI (all items less fresh 
food) was likely to be at around 2 percent in the near term, as the rise in energy prices was 
likely to be curbed by the government's fuel oil subsidies and its measures in summer 2026 
to reduce the household burden of higher electricity and gas charges. Toward the second half 
of fiscal 2026, however, the rate of increase in this CPI was expected to accelerate markedly, 
to a level exceeding 3 percent, due to a greater upward contribution of energy prices reflecting

7 
a rise in electricity and gas charges driven by higher import prices of liquefied natural gas 
(LNG), as well as a higher rate of increase in the CPI (all items less fresh food and energy). 
 
2. Financial environment 
Japan's financial conditions had been accommodative. 
Real interest rates remained at low levels, mainly in the short- to medium-term zone, 
although they had recently risen. Firms' funding costs had increased. Firms' demand for funds 
had increased on the back of, for example, the recovery in economic activity  as well as 
mergers and acquisitions of firms. With regard to credit supply, financial institutions' lending 
attitudes as perceived by firms had been accommodative. Issuance conditions for CP and 
corporate bonds had been favorable. In this situation, the year-on-year rate of increase in the 
amount outstanding of bank lending had been in the range of 5.5 -6.0 percent; that in the 
aggregate amount outstanding of CP and corporate bonds had been at around 5 percent. Firms' 
financial positions had been favorable. The number of bankruptcies of firms had been more 
or less flat. 
Meanwhile, the year-on-year rate of change in the money stock had been at around 
2 percent. 
 
II. Changes to the Terms and Conditions of the Funds-Supplying Operations to Support 
Financing for Climate Change Responses 
A. Staff Reports 
With regard to the Funds -Supplying Operations to Support Financing for Climate 
Change Responses, since the start of lending in December 2021, the outstanding balance had 
continued to increase, on the back of steady progress in private sector efforts to add ress 
climate change and financial institutions significantly revising their target levels upward. 
Given these developments, it could be assessed that the purpose of the operations had been 
achieved thus far. On this basis, with roughly half of the implemen tation period -- which is 
until the end of fiscal 2030 -- having already elapsed, it would be appropriate that the Bank, 
with the aim of continuing to provide stable support for private sector efforts on climate 
change while ensuring the smooth conduct of market operations, amend the "Principal Terms 
and Conditions of the Funds-Supplying Operations to Support Financing for Climate Change 
Responses" as follows: the Bank would (1) change the loan rate from the current fixed rate

8 
to a floating rate and (2) set an upper limit on the total amount of loans at 50 trillion yen and 
on the amount of loans for each counterparty at 10 trillion yen. 
Regarding the Funds -Supplying Operation to Support Financial Institutions in 
Disaster Areas, it would be appropriate that the Bank also change the loan rate from the 
current fixed rate to a floating rate, together with the changes regarding the Funds-Supplying 
Operations to Support Financing for Climate Change Responses. Moreover, with regard to 
the loan rate for the Funds -Supplying Operations against Pooled Collateral, it would be 
desirable that the Bank, with the aim of facilitating money market operation s, enable the 
selection of the floating -rate method, in addition to the current multiple -rate competitive 
auction method and fixed-rate method. 
With regard to the Great East Japan Earthquake -- the designated disaster under the 
Funds-Supplying Operation to Support Financial Institutions in Disaster Areas -- it was 
deemed that the purpose of the operation to support efforts toward restoration and 
reconstruction made by financial institutions in disaster areas had been achieved, given 
factors such as the recent usage of the operation. It would therefore be appropriate that the 
Bank amend the "Principal Terms and Conditions for the Funds -Supplying Ope ration to 
Support Financial Institutions in Disaster Areas" and remove the Great East Japan Earthquake 
from the category of designated disasters under the operation following the loan disbursement 
in May 2027. 
 
B. Discussions by the Policy Board 
Members agreed that, as explained by the staff, it was appropriate that the Bank 
would (1) change the loan rate and set upper limits on the amount of loans under the Funds -
Supplying Operations to Support Financing for Climate Change Responses, (2) change t he 
loan rates for the Funds -Supplying Operation to Support Financial Institutions in Disaster 
Areas and the Funds -Supplying Operations against Pooled Collateral, and (3) remove the 
Great East Japan Earthquake from the category of designated disasters under  the Funds -
Supplying Operation to Support Financial Institutions in Disaster Areas following the loan 
disbursement in May 2027. 
A few members expressed the recognition that the changes to the loan rates 
discussed at this meeting were timely, as they would provide appropriate usage incentives to

9 
financial institutions, depending on economic and financial developments, while the Bank 
would make necessary adjustments to the policy interest rate. 
 
C. Vote 
Members voted unanimously to approve the amendments. It was confirmed that this 
would be made public after the meeting. 
 
III. Summary of Discussions by the Policy Board on Economic and Financial 
Developments 
A. Economic and Price Developments 
With regard to global financial and capital markets, members shared the view that, 
although high uncertainties over the situation in the Middle East remained, market sentiment 
continued to be at an improved level, as robust corporate profits had been maintained. 
Members shared the recognition that overseas economies had grown moderately on 
the whole, although some weakness had been seen in part, partly due to the impact of the 
situation in the Middle East. As for the outlook, members agreed that overseas economies 
were expected to come under downward pressure for the time being due to the impact of the 
situation in the Middle East. They continued that, thereafter, however, based on the 
assumption that the impact of the situation in the Middle East would ease, overseas economies 
were projected to continue growing moderately, partly supported by global AI-related demand. 
One member said that, since the previous meeting, sentiment toward the strength in and the 
sustainability of AI-related demand had further improved. The member then pointed out that, 
amid such a demand shock and the rise in global inflationary pressure, global monetary policy 
had been shifting away from the previous trend of policy interest rate cuts observed from 
2024 to 2025, and moving toward a policy of interest rate hikes from summer 2026. 
Members agreed that the U.S. economy maintained solid growth on the whole, 
although some weakness had been seen in part. One member expressed the view that the 
economy was expected to recover, supported by fiscal spending -- including tax cuts and tariff 
revenue refunds -- and by the wealth effects from higher stock prices, mainly of AI -related 
firms, in addition to employment conditions heading toward improvement, amid an 
improvement in the terms of trade due to the United States being a net exporter of cr ude oil. 
One member pointed out  that, although the U.S. economy was expected to exhibit solid

10 
growth of slightly more than 2 percent, attention needed to be paid to uncertainties 
surrounding future economic policy , taking into account  factors such as  concerns over 
medium- to long-term fiscal sustainability and the effects of the U.S. midterm elections in 
November. 
Members shared the recognition that European economies remained resilient on the 
whole, but downward pressure on domestic demand had been observed, reflecting the impact 
of the situation in the Middle East. 
Members shared the view that the Chinese economy had picked up on the whole, 
mainly supported by the increase in exports, although consumption had lacked momentum. 
One member expressed the recognition that , despite subdued private consumption and 
business fixed investment due to a decline in real estate prices and a deterioration in local 
government finances, the Chinese economy maintained a certain level of growth, driven by 
the increase in exports and by support from fiscal policy. 
Members shared the recognition that growth in emerging and commodity-exporting 
economies other than China had improved moderately on the whole, although some weakness 
had been seen in part. 
Based on the above deliberations on economic and financial conditions abroad, 
members discussed the state of Japan's economy. 
With regard to economic activity, members shared the view that Japan's economy 
had recovered moderately, although some weakness had been seen in part, partly due to the 
impact of the situation in the Middle East. Most members shared the view that, although the 
situation in the Middle East remained unclear, the economy had been supported by progress 
in securing alternative sources of supply and by the government's various measures. These 
members continued that, under these circumstances, AI -related demand ha d spread in the 
corporate sector, with its positive impact being observed in exports, production, and profits, 
and that private consumption had been resilient in the household sector, against the 
background of improvement in the employment and income situa tion. In this regard, these 
members expressed the recognition that Japan's economy had been developing generally in 
line with the baseline scenario presented in the July 2026 Outlook for Economic Activity and 
Prices (Outlook Report). One of these members p ointed out that, although the rise in prices 
of crude oil and other imported commodities was a factor that led to a deterioration in the 
terms of trade, both the price and volume of exports from Japan had risen, partly driven by

11 
the demand shock from the global expansion in AI -related demand, and that, under these 
circumstances, the deterioration in the terms of trade in the current phase had been limited on 
the whole, and concerns over an economic slowdown had subsided. Meanwhile, one member 
expressed the recognition that, although the real GDP growth rate for the April -June quarter 
had been positive, the breakdown showed that the growth rate for domestic demand was 
negative, and while external demand was positive, this was merely the result of a decrease in 
imports reflecting supply -side constraints stemming from the situation in the Middle East. 
The member continued that, therefore, it could not be said that the economy was growing in 
a strong and sound manner. In response, a few members pointed out that the somewhat weak 
figures for domestic demand in the April -June GDP statistics were considered to be 
attributable to technical factors regarding the treatment of, for example, the sale of intellectual 
property rights abroad and th e provision of free school lunches. These members continued 
that, considering these factors, domestic demand was not weak, and the economy had been 
developing in line with the projections made in the July 2026 Outlook Report. 
As for the outlook for economic activity , members shared the recognition that  (1) 
Japan's economy was expected to continue growing moderately, albeit at a decelerated 
rate -- this was because, although the rise in crude oil prices since early spring reflecting the 
impact of the situation in the Middle East was expected to push down economic activity, the 
economy was likely to be underpinned by factors such as the government's various measures 
and accommodative financial conditions, in addition to the increase in global AI -related 
demand and (2) Japan's economic growth rate was likely to rise moderately thereafter, since 
it was projected  that the adverse effects of high crude oil prices would wane and that the 
virtuous cycle from income to spending would gradually intensify. 
Members agreed that exports and industrial production had started to increase, albeit 
moderately, against the backdrop of factors such as the solid increase in global AI-related 
demand. One member noted that the breakdown of the coincident index of the Indexes of 
Business Conditions for July showed an increase in shipments related to industrial production, 
particularly shipment s of investment goods related to semiconductors , and the member 
expected these developments to continue. 
Members shared the recognition that business fixed investment had been on a 
moderate increasing trend, despite being affected by the rise in construction costs, with 
corporate profits being at high levels, against the backdrop of factors such as the solid increase

12 
in global AI-related demand. One member pointed out that real business fixed investment in 
the GDP statistics had registered negative growth for two consecutive quarters -- despite the 
recent temporary fluctuations stemming from the sale of intellectual pr operty rights 
abroad -- which suggested that investment had been sluggish relative to the active plans, and 
thus attention was warranted on future downside risks. In response, one member stated that 
the weak business fixed investment relative to the plans was mainly due to supply-side factors 
such as the rise in construction costs and labor shortages, and considering that order backlogs 
for investment should be cleared over time, there was no need to be excessively concerned. 
A different member pointed out that, according to the Survey on Planned Capital Spending 
conducted by the Development Bank of Japan (DBJ), investment related to AI and data 
centers was expected across a wide range of manufacturing industries, and an increase in 
investment related to ele ctricity had also been seen in nonmanufacturing industries as an 
inducement effect of AI-related demand. 
Most members shared the recognition that private consumption had been resilient 
against the background of the improvement in the employment and income situation, 
although weakness had been seen in household sentiment. Some members pointed out that, 
with corporate profits being at high levels, real wages had improved and bonus payments this 
summer had been solid, and that, under these circumstances, consumer sentiment -- which 
had been relatively weak -- had picked up and private consumption had shown resili ence. 
One member expressed the view that real private consumption in the April -June GDP 
statistics remained unchanged from the previous quarter, showing a lack of momentum. In 
relation to this, one member noted that private consumption not having been nece ssarily 
strong was attributable to structural factors, such as the population decline and the current 
pension benefit system. The member then expressed the view that, from a cyclical perspective, 
private consumption had been rather resilient, partly due to  wage increases and the 
government's various measures to transfer income to households. A different member added 
that, looking at developments in real disposable income, while property income and the 
compensation of employees had increased, social insurance benefits, including pensions, had 
not grown. The member continued that, as a result, consumption behavior had varied 
considerably depending on household attributes, such as generation and the composition of 
financial assets held.

Members shared the view that the employment and income situation had improved 
moderately. One member said that the rate of increase in the CPI for 2026 was expected to be 
lower than the past few years on a calendar-year basis, and the impact on firms' wage setting 
warranted attention. The member then expressed  the view that, considering that robust 
corporate profits had been maintained, it was highly possible  at this point that the wage 
increases in the annual spring labor-management wage negotiations in 2027 would be similar 
to the figures seen in 2026. 
As for prices, members shared the recognition that, despite factors such as the effects 
of the government's measures to reduce the household burden of higher energy prices, the 
year-on-year rate of increase in the CPI (all items less fresh food) had been moderately rising 
recently, being in the range of 1.5 -2.0 percent, as the effects of a rise in import prices had 
been emerging, with moves to pass on wage increases to selling prices continuing. One 
member pointed out that, although the year-on-year rate of increase in the CPI had been rising 
moderately, the rate of increase in not only the CPI (all items less fresh food) but also the CPI 
(all items less fresh food and energy) remained in the range of 1.5 -2.0 percent recently. In 
response, some members said that upward pressure on prices in business -to-business 
transactions had started to spill over into consumer prices, and that the month-on-month rate 
of increase in the CPI (all items less fresh food and energy) had accelerated. A different 
member expressed the recognition that, within the CPI, the year -on-year rate of increase in 
services prices had been rising gradually thus far, but it appeared that the pace of increase had 
been more or less unchanged recently, and that the figures did not clearly suggest  a further 
acceleration in services prices. In response, some members pointed out that services prices 
continued to increase steadily over the past few years, albeit gradually. These members then 
said that if this trend accelerated further, underlying infl ation might deviate upward 
accordingly. One of these members noted that, when assessing the underlying trend in 
services prices, it was desirable to exclude housing rent and public services, as price revisions 
for these items tend to lag. The member contin ued that the year -on-year rate of increase in 
services prices excluding housing rent and public services had already been at around 3 
percent for nearly two years, and that the current phase should rather be focused on ensuring 
this rate did not accelerate. 
Regarding price developments in business -to-business transactions, members 
concurred that the rate of increase in the PPI continued to be high on a year -on-year basis,

14 
reflecting the impact of the expansion in AI -related demand, in addition to high crude oil 
prices and the depreciation of the yen. As for the breakdown of the PPI, a few members 
pointed out that, while the momentum of price hikes at the upstream stage in the supply chain, 
such as for petroleum and coal products, had paused, the price pass -through of these hikes 
had been spreading to the midstream and downstream stages, for example, for plastic products. 
On this basis, one of these members expressed the view that, with the preliminary figure for 
the year-on-year rate of increase in the PPI registering 7.6 percent for August, there were 
views that the rate of increase had peaked out from the revised figure for July, which was 7.7 
percent. The member continued that, however, given that the preliminary figure for July had 
been revised upward to a considerable extent in the following month, it was quite possible 
that the preliminary figure for August would also be revised upward. With regard to 
developments in the SPPI, one member noted that the year-on-year rate of increase for motor 
freight transportation, categorized under domestic distribution costs, had accelerated to 
almost 6 percent for July. On this basis, the member expressed the view that, considering that 
gasoline prices had been declining on a year-on-year basis, it was likely that there had been a 
de facto pass-through of increases in personnel expenses, triggered by high crude oil prices. 
Meanwhile, members concurred that inflation expectations had risen moderately.  
One member expressed the recognition that, as the accumulated experience of inflation since 
2022 had led to an increase in medium- to long-term inflation expectations, the recent rise in 
crude oil prices had been driving a further rise in inflation expectations. One member pointed 
out that households' perceived inflation had recently been easing somewhat, with rice prices 
declining, while active price pass-through continued in business-to-business transactions. The 
member then commented that, in assessing developments in inflation expectations, it was 
important to examine moves by various economic entities, including firms and households. 
With regard to the outlook for prices, members shared the recognition that the year-
on-year rate of increase in the CPI (all items less fresh food) was likely to accelerate to a level 
clearly above 2 percent from the second half of fiscal 2026. They continued that, this was 
because, with moves to pass on wage increases to selling prices continuing, the rise in crude 
oil prices to date was expected to push up prices, mainly of energy and goods, and the rise in 
the prices of semiconductors and other items, re flecting the increase in global AI -related 
demand, and the recent depreciation of the yen were likely to lead to an increase in prices, 
mainly of durable goods. In addition, members agreed that, meanwhile, with a sense of labor
