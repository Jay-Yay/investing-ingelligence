 DoorDash Releases Third Quarter 2026 Financial Results 

 October 29, 2026 

 SAN FRANCISCO—(BUSINESS WIRE)—DoorDash, Inc. (NASDAQ: DASH) today announced its financial results for the quarter ended September 30, 2026. 

 In Q3 2026, we generated new quarterly records across Marketplace GOV, revenue, and net income. In the quarter, we drove continued growth in our membership 1 programs, launched new products, added more selection, advanced our global technology platform, and continued to innovate in key new areas of our business, including merchant services, AI, and autonomy. We are pleased with our execution through the first nine months of 2026 and excited about the contributions we are making to local economies. 

 Additionally, we announced a definitive agreement to acquire Maplebear Inc. ("Instacart") for $65.00 per share, payable in at least 75% cash and up to 25% DoorDash common stock, subject to election and proration mechanics. We believe that, together with Instacart, our combined scale and complementary technologies will enable us to build the next generation of tools and services that will empower local merchants to thrive, provide more choice for consumers, and generate more opportunity for Dashers 2 and shoppers using Instacart's platform ("Shoppers"). 

 Third Quarter 2026 Key Financial Metrics 

 • Total Orders increased 28% year-over-year (Y/Y) to 993 million. 

 • Marketplace GOV increased 36% Y/Y to $33.9 billion. 

 • Revenue increased 34% Y/Y to $4.6 billion. 

 • GAAP net income attributable to DoorDash, Inc. common stockholders increased 91% Y/Y to $466 million. 

 • Adjusted EBITDA increased 41% Y/Y to $1,060 million.

 Operational Update 

 In Q3 2026, we grew Total Orders 28% Y/Y (17% Y/Y excluding the impact of Deliveroo), Marketplace GOV 36% Y/Y (23% Y/Y excluding the impact of Deliveroo), and revenue 34% Y/Y (23% Y/Y excluding the impact of Deliveroo). In Q3 2026, net income attributable to our common stockholders was $466 million and Adjusted EBITDA was $1,060 million. As a note, beginning in Q4 2026, we intend to stop disclosing quarterly results excluding Deliveroo, as this will represent the anniversary of the acquisition and our total company results will be largely comparable with the year-ago period. 

 In our U.S. restaurant category in Q3 2026, Y/Y growth in Marketplace GOV accelerated slightly compared to Q2 2026, despite a challenging Y/Y comparison due to strong growth in Q3 2025. On a quarter-over-quarter (Q/Q) basis, Marketplace GOV in the U.S. restaurant category grew faster in Q3 2026 than in any third quarter since Q3 2020. Growth in the U.S. restaurant category was driven by continued Y/Y growth in order rates 3 among mature cohorts 4 and accelerated Y/Y growth in new consumers. 5 We continued to drive strong growth in U.S. DashPass paid memberships in Q3 2026, which helps reduce transactional friction for consumers. Over the last two years, growth in DashPass has helped drive a 9% reduction in transaction fees per order in the U.S. restaurant category. 

 In our U.S. grocery and retail categories, Y/Y growth in Marketplace GOV in Q3 2026 remained strong, growing at more than double the pace of Y/Y growth in our U.S. restaurant category. During the quarter, in our U.S. grocery and retail categories, we announced several new merchant partners, supporting broader selection for consumers, and we continued to drive improvements to affordability and quality, which helped drive continued double-digit Y/Y growth in order rates for nearly all mature cohorts in Q3 2026, as well as Y/Y growth in new consumers to our U.S. grocery and retail categories that was consistent with Y/Y growth in the first half of 2026. 

 We view the relatively consistent growth in our U.S. marketplace in Q3 2026, and over the last four years, as a positive signal of underlying demand trends and a reflection of our ability to continue producing increased value through product innovation. A recent addition to our service in some cities is reservations and, during Q3 2026, restaurant reservations through our U.S. marketplace grew over 340% Q/Q in existing geographies, with more than 10% of reservations in Q3 2026 coming from consumers who were new to DoorDash in the quarter. 6 

 In our international marketplaces, we drove accelerated Y/Y growth in Marketplace GOV in Q3 2026, 7 supported by accelerated Y/Y growth in Total Orders at each of our international brands. We continue to diffuse operating insights and product innovations across the company, which is helping improve the efficiency of our international operations. At the same time, we continue to work to improve the dynamism of our capital allocation across geographies and product areas, so that we can take advantage of the best opportunities we see to positively impact customer outcomes and drive long-term profit production. We believe these improvements helped enable us to drive strong topline performance in our international marketplaces in Q3 2026, with contributions to total profitability that exceeded our expectations entering the quarter. 

 The first nine months of 2026 was a period of excellent execution and solid financial performance. Nonetheless, we continue to have significant work ahead of us to improve our existing services, complete the implementation of our new global technology platform, and build the next generation of services that will drive local commerce and our company forward. 

 Agreement to Acquire Instacart 

 DoorDash, Inc. and Instacart today announced a definitive agreement under which DoorDash will acquire Instacart for a total value of $65.00 for each Instacart share, or an equity value of approximately $17 billion. Instacart stockholders will have the right to elect to receive, for each Instacart share: (i) $65.00 in cash, (ii) 0.3372 shares of DoorDash common stock, or (iii) a mix of $48.75 in cash and 0.0843 shares of DoorDash common stock, in each case, subject to a cap on the total stock consideration issuable by DoorDash equal to 25% of the aggregate consideration and certain proration mechanics. Instacart's board of directors has unanimously approved the agreement.

 The value of $65.00 per Instacart share represents a 26% premium to Instacart's closing price on October 22, 2026, the last trading day before media reports about a possible transaction. As described above, Instacart stockholders may elect to receive cash, DoorDash common stock or a mix of cash and DoorDash common stock for each Instacart share, subject to a cap on the total stock consideration issuable by DoorDash equal to 25% of the aggregate consideration. If stock consideration is oversubscribed, applicable Instacart stockholders will receive stock consideration on a prorated basis. 

 DoorDash expects to fund the cash portion of the consideration with cash on hand and proceeds from debt financing. DoorDash has entered into a financing commitment letter with JPMorgan Chase Bank, N.A. providing for a 364-day senior unsecured bridge loan facility in support of the debt component of the transaction, subject to satisfaction or waiver of certain customary conditions. The transaction is not subject to a financing condition. DoorDash is targeting a capital structure that will maintain investment-grade ratings. 

 The transaction is expected to close in the end of 2027 or the first half of 2028, subject to approval by Instacart stockholders, the expiration or termination of the waiting period under the Hart-Scott-Rodino Antitrust Improvements Act of 1976, other regulatory approvals, including in Canada and Israel, and other customary closing conditions. Stockholders holding about 19% of Instacart's outstanding shares have agreed to vote in favor of the transaction. 

 Grocery Delivery and Instacart 

 Grocery delivery is still early in its adoption. We’ve made meaningful progress over the past five years at DoorDash, significantly improving logistics quality for grocery delivery, but as we’ve said many times, the product still isn’t there. Consumers are still asked to pay a premium for imperfect delivery. 

 To achieve the end state of perfect grocery delivery every time at the best possible prices, we believe that invention is required on the fulfillment and business models of grocery delivery. In order to obtain perfect delivery every time from the widest selection of grocers, we believe managing the inventory ourselves for third-party grocers is important. This is why we started our DashMart project five years ago, first learning how to do this by holding our own inventory, and now managing thousands of high-quality products from Giant Eagle, Albertsons, and others. When we manage inventory ourselves, we achieve 99%+ fulfillment accuracy. 8 We also deliver more than 10 minutes faster on average, 9 because the warehouse environment is optimized for delivery whereas a grocery store was never set up for delivery. In addition to faster delivery, managing the inventory ourselves gives us the potential to reduce the cost of fulfillment, and lower costs enable us to reinvest into more value for consumers. 

 Managing inventory inside a warehouse is a fixed cost business. Scale is a necessary (though insufficient) input into helping lower costs. This is one of the reasons we’re attracted to Instacart, as it adds scale that is unique to our network. 

 Over the past 14 years, Instacart has built a great brand that serves millions of consumers. Hence, we believe that combining our reach will enable the best possible logistics and software infrastructure to produce the best possible grocery delivery experience for grocers, consumers, Shoppers, and advertising partners. We expect this to be true on Day 1 (after close), as the combined reach will increase density across the grocery store network, helping increase delivery speed, lower fulfillment costs, and provide greater earning opportunities for Shoppers. 

 As we have historically done, we expect to reinvest a portion of the improved network efficiencies into creating a better experience for all audiences. We’ve done this both with our U.S. restaurant marketplace, where our reinvestments into product innovation have continued to generate durable growth, and in our acquired businesses outside of the U.S. Most recently, we demonstrated this investment philosophy and approach at Deliveroo, which in Q3 2026, saw Total Orders grow at twice the Y/Y rate it did in Q3 2025 (pre-acquisition). 

 To this end, we see a multi-year path to $500 million or more of annual synergies, net of investments. We expect to invest more than 100% of total synergies in the first year after close, followed by positive net synergies in year two and beyond. However, our initial aims in online grocery with third-party fulfillment won’t be the end state, and Instacart’s scale will benefit us on the journey toward creating a better product experience for grocery delivery. First, the expected efficiencies

 created can give us the investment capacity to invest further in our fulfillment network. Second, Instacart’s scale will help amortize the fixed cost of managing inventory on behalf of grocers. 

 Moreover, Instacart shares the same mission as we do to empower merchants to grow on their own channels. Instacart has invested in its Enterprise product for several years and is a leader in this space.  Finally, the combined reach and cross selling opportunities across different categories will be a win for advertisers who are always seeking scaled platforms to earn the best return on ad spend. 

 Atoms vs Bits 

 Four years ago, as ChatGPT launched and the advent of the AI revolution was getting started, we recognized two important challenges for companies: one to be the leading digital personal assistant, and the other to become the leading physical network to help fulfill tasks in the real world. DoorDash will be focused on the second of these two battles. Building a scaled logistics network and solving the challenges that connect all of these audiences in the end-to-end flow of delivery is how we intend to compete. 

 As AI personal agents are now starting to become more mainstream, our strategy today remains focused on winning the battle for atoms. This doesn’t mean that we won’t contribute to AI innovation digitally. In fact, we recently shipped a series of AI agents that solve real customer problems including: Ask DoorDash, which helps you find new restaurants most similar to what your usual orders are and enables you to build your grocery order baskets faster; Text DoorDash, a texting solution that is the fastest way to order outside the DoorDash app; and a Model Context Protocol (MCP) that interfaces with external AI agents, including those used at companies for easier corporate ordering. 

 But from a capital allocation perspective, we are investing substantial sums in two ways to build for the physical world. We are investing in building capabilities like warehousing and in autonomous delivery. We are also investing in our ability to help merchants win with unique demand and the best products, such as today’s announcement to team up with Instacart.

 Use of Non-GAAP Financial Measures 

 To supplement our financial information presented in accordance with accounting principles generally accepted in the United States of America ("GAAP"), we consider certain financial measures that are not prepared in accordance with GAAP, including adjusted cost of revenue, adjusted sales and marketing expense, adjusted research and development expense, adjusted general and administrative expense, Adjusted Gross Profit, Adjusted Gross Margin, Contribution Profit, Contribution Margin, Adjusted EBITDA, Free Cash Flow, and revenue, excluding Deliveroo. We use these financial measures in conjunction with GAAP measures as part of our overall assessment of our performance, including the preparation of our annual operating budget and quarterly forecasts, to evaluate the effectiveness of our business strategies and to communicate with our board of directors concerning our business and financial performance. We believe that these non-GAAP financial measures provide useful information to investors about our business and financial performance, enhance their overall understanding of our past performance and future prospects, and allow for greater transparency with respect to metrics used by our management in their financial and operational decision making. We are presenting these non-GAAP financial measures to assist investors in seeing our business and financial performance through the eyes of management, and because we believe that these non-GAAP financial measures provide an additional tool for investors to use in comparing results of operations of our business over multiple periods and with other companies in our industry. 

 We define adjusted cost of revenue as cost of revenue, exclusive of depreciation and amortization, excluding stock-based compensation expense and certain payroll tax expense, allocated overhead, and inventory write-off related to 

 9 

 

 
 

 restructuring. Allocated overhead is determined based on an allocation of shared costs, such as facilities (including rent and utilities) and information technology costs, among all departments based on employee headcount. We define adjusted sales and marketing expense as sales and marketing expenses excluding stock-based compensation expense and certain payroll tax expense, and allocated overhead. We define adjusted research and development expense as research and development expenses excluding stock-based compensation expense and certain payroll tax expense, and allocated overhead. We define adjusted general and administrative expense as general and administrative expenses excluding stock-based compensation expense and certain payroll tax expense, certain legal, tax, and regulatory settlements, reserves, and expenses, transaction-related costs (primarily consists of acquisition, integration, and investment related costs), impairment expenses, and including allocated overhead from cost of revenue, sales and marketing, and research and development. 

 We define Adjusted Gross Profit as gross profit plus (i) depreciation and amortization expense related to cost of revenue, (ii) stock-based compensation expense and certain payroll tax expense included in cost of revenue, (iii) allocated overhead included in cost of revenue, and (iv) inventory write-off related to restructuring. Gross profit is defined as revenue less (i) cost of revenue, exclusive of depreciation and amortization and (ii) depreciation and amortization related to cost of revenue. Adjusted Gross Margin is defined as Adjusted Gross Profit as a percentage of revenue for the same period. 

 We define Contribution Profit as our gross profit less sales and marketing expense plus (i) depreciation and amortization expense related to cost of revenue, (ii) stock-based compensation expense and certain payroll tax expense included in cost of revenue and sales and marketing expenses, (iii) allocated overhead included in cost of revenue and sales and marketing expenses, and (iv) inventory write-off related to restructuring. We define gross margin as gross profit as a percentage of revenue for the same period and we define Contribution Margin as Contribution Profit as a percentage of revenue for the same period. We use Contribution Profit to evaluate our operating performance and trends. We believe that Contribution Profit is a useful indicator of the economic impact of orders fulfilled through DoorDash as it takes into account the direct expenses associated with generating and fulfilling orders. 

 Adjusted EBITDA is a measure that we use to assess our operating performance and the operating leverage in our business. We define Adjusted EBITDA as net income (loss) attributable to DoorDash, Inc. common stockholders, adjusted to include net income (loss) attributable to redeemable non-controlling interests and exclude (i) certain legal, tax, and regulatory settlements, reserves, and expenses, (ii) loss on disposal of property and equipment, (iii) transaction-related costs (primarily consists of acquisition, integration, and investment related costs), (iv) impairment expenses, (v) restructuring charges, (vi) inventory write-off related to restructuring, (vii) provision for (benefit from) income taxes, (viii) interest income, net, (ix) other (income) expense, net, (x) stock-based compensation expense and certain payroll tax expense, and (xi) depreciation and amortization expense. 

 We define Free Cash Flow as cash flows from operating activities less purchases of property and equipment and capitalized software and website development costs. 

 We define Total Orders as all orders completed through our marketplaces and Commerce Platform over the period of measurement. 

 We define Marketplace GOV as the total dollar value of orders completed on our marketplaces, including taxes, tips, and any applicable consumer fees, including membership fees related to DashPass, Wolt+, and Deliveroo Plus. Marketplace GOV does not include the dollar value of orders, taxes and tips, or fees charged to merchants, for orders fulfilled through our Commerce Platform. 

 We define Net Revenue Margin as revenue expressed as a percentage of Marketplace GOV. 

 We define revenue, excluding Deliveroo as revenue, excluding the revenue attributable to our Deliveroo branded marketplaces and Commerce Platform. We believe that revenue, excluding Deliveroo is a useful metric for period-over-period comparability of our legacy business. 

 Our definitions may differ from the definitions used by other companies and therefore comparability may be limited. In addition, other companies may not publish these or similar metrics. Further, these metrics have certain limitations in that they do not include the impact of certain expenses that are reflected in our consolidated statements of operations.
