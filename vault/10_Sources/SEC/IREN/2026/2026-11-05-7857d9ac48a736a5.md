 GLOSSARY OF INDUSTRY TERMS AND CONCEPTS 

 This Quarterly Report includes a number of industry terms and concepts which are defined as follows: 

 • AI Cloud : The Group’s offering platforms that support Artificial Intelligence (“AI”) workloads such as training and inference through cloud-based infrastructure. 

 • AI Cloud Lease : a financial reporting classification for arrangements within the Group’s AI Cloud business that are accounted for as leases under ASC 842 Leases , as described in the Group’s Annual Report and in Note 2 to the unaudited condensed consolidated financial statements included in this Quarterly Report. The classification reflects the accounting treatment of those arrangements and does not denote a separate business, segment or service offering. 

 • AI Cloud Services : A financial reporting classification for arrangements within the Group’s AI Cloud business that are accounted for as service revenue under ASC 606 Revenue from Contracts with Customers , as described in the Group's Annual Report and in Note 2 to the unaudited condensed consolidated financial statements included in this Quarterly Report. The classification reflects the accounting treatment of those arrangements and does not denote a separate business, segment or service offering. 

 • ASICs : An Application Specific Integrated Circuit is a type of integrated circuit that is custom-designed for a particular use, rather than intended for general-purpose use. 

 • Board : The board of directors of IREN Limited. 

 • Co-Founders and Co-Chief Executive Officers: Daniel Roberts and William Roberts. 

 • EH/s : Exahash per second. 1 EH/s equals one quintillion hashes per second (1,000,000,000,000,000,000 h/s). 

 • ERCOT : the Electric Reliability Council of Texas, which operates the electrical grid serving most of Texas. 

 • GPUs : Graphics processing units are a type of computing technology designed for parallel processing, which can be used in a wide range of applications, including graphics and video rendering, gaming, creative production and AI. 

 • GW : Gigawatts. 1GW equals 1,000 megawatts. 

 • Hashrate : a measure of the computational power used to mine and process transactions on a proof-of-work network such as Bitcoin. 

 • HPC : High-performance computing, the use of aggregated computing resources to solve complex computational problems, including AI workloads. 

 • MW : Megawatts. 1MW equals 1,000 kilowatts. 

 • REC : Renewable Energy Certificate. 

 • SEC : U.S. Securities and Exchange Commission.

 Certain customer contracts include significant advance prepayments. For arrangements accounted for under ASC Topic 606, Revenue from Contracts with Customers (“ASC 606”), the Group assesses at contract inception whether the payment terms provide a significant financing benefit. This assessment considers the interval between payment and delivery of the related services, any difference between the contractual consideration and the cash selling price, prevailing market interest rates and the commercial reasons for the payment terms. As a practical expedient, the Group does not adjust consideration for financing effects where it expects, at contract inception, that the interval between payment and delivery of the corresponding services will be one year or less. Where a contract is determined to contain a significant financing component, the resulting interest expense is presented separately from revenue within finance expense in the Unaudited Condensed Consolidated Statements of Operations and Comprehensive Income (Loss). 

 Advance payments attributable to arrangements accounted for under ASC 842 are recognized as deferred lease revenue and are subject to the Group’s lease accounting policy. 

 AI Cloud Lease revenue 

 At contract inception, the Group assesses whether an arrangement is, or contains, a lease. An arrangement contains a lease where it conveys the right to control the use of an identified asset for a period of time in exchange for consideration. Where the Group is the lessor, each lease is classified at commencement as a sales-type, direct financing or operating lease. Leases that do not meet the criteria for sales-type or direct financing classification, and leases with variable payments not based on an index or rate for which such classification would result in a selling loss at commencement, are classified as operating leases. For operating leases, the underlying assets remain within property, plant and equipment and continue to be depreciated, and no net investment in the lease is recognized.

 Lease commencement is the date on which the underlying asset is made available for the customer’s use.  Finite-lived intangible assets 

 Intangible assets acquired in a business combination are initially recognized at fair value as of the acquisition date. 

 Developed technology and customer contracts and related relationships are classified as finite-lived intangible assets and are amortized on a straight-line basis over the shorter of their contractual term and their estimated period of economic benefit. The Group reviews the useful lives, residual values and amortization methods at least annually, and adjusts them prospectively if expectations change. 

 In-process research and development (“IPR&D”) represents acquired projects that have not yet reached technological feasibility. IPR&D is indefinite-lived and not amortized until the related project is completed and the technology is available for its intended use, at which point it is reclassified to developed technology and amortized over its determined useful life. Subsequent development costs are expensed as incurred and abandoned projects are written off. 

 Finite-lived intangible assets are carried at cost (or acquisition-date fair value) less accumulated amortization and impairment, and are evaluated for impairment whenever events or changes in circumstances indicate that the carrying amount may not be recoverable. When such indicators exist, the Group compares the carrying amount of the asset (or asset group) with its estimated undiscounted future cash flows. If the carrying amount exceeds the undiscounted cash flows, an impairment loss is recognized for the excess of the carrying amount over the asset’s fair value, with the loss recorded in “Impairment of assets” in the Condensed Consolidated Statements of Operations and Comprehensive Income (Loss).

 Note 14. Fair value measurement 

 Assets and liabilities that are measured in the Condensed Consolidated Balance Sheets at fair value are categorized into a three-level hierarchy based on the priority of the inputs to the valuation. The categorization within the hierarchy is based on the lowest level input that is significant to the fair value measurement, being: 

 Level 1: Quoted prices (unadjusted) in active markets for identical assets or liabilities that the entity can access at the measurement date. 

 Level 2: Inputs other than quoted prices included within Level 1 that are observable for the asset or liability, either directly or indirectly. 

 Level 3: Unobservable inputs for the asset or liability.

 Overview 

 IREN is a vertically integrated AI Cloud platform, delivering data centers, compute and software for AI training and inference. 

 We own and operate all three layers of the AI Cloud stack. The data center layer includes the land, power, substations, buildings and cooling that form the physical foundation of our AI Cloud platform. The compute layer includes the GPUs, CPUs, storage, servers and networking deployed within that data center infrastructure. The software layer includes the managed services and enterprise support that enable customers to deploy, operate and manage AI workloads. 

 We generate AI Cloud revenue by delivering both bare metal compute and managed cloud services to customers supporting AI training and inference workloads. Our customer base includes hyperscalers, enterprises, AI developers, frontier labs, and channel partners. As of September 30, 2026, our operating AI Cloud capacity represented approximately 93MW.

 Bitcoin mining 

 We continued to operate Bitcoin miners at our data centers during the three months ended September 30, 2026.  Bitcoin mining revenue is affected principally by the market price of Bitcoin, the global network hashrate, and the block reward. The block reward is subject to halving at predetermined intervals; the most recent halving occurred in April 2024, reducing the reward to 3.125 Bitcoin per block, and the next is expected in April 2028. Our mining revenue is also affected by the cost and availability of electricity at the relevant sites, and by the efficiency of our mining fleet.
