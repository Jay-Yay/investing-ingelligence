CoWoS Capacity Gap Narrows; TSMC Warns ABF Substrates Will Become the Next Bottleneck in Advanced Packaging
TSMC (2330.TW) Vice President of Advanced Packaging Technology and Services, He Jun, stated at the OCP APAC Summit on the 11th that with CoWoS capacity doubling for three consecutive years, supply is now "very close" to market demand. However, the advanced packaging supply chain bottleneck is shifting from memory to ABF substrates, which could become the second most critical constraint on AI chip expansion in the coming years, trailing only memory.
He Jun, often referred to as "Mr. CoWoS," noted during the summit that the relentless demand for AI computing power is driving rapid evolution in advanced packaging architectures. The 5.5x reticle size CoWoS is now in high-volume production, with yields consistently exceeding 98% across multiple AI customer products, and some even reaching 99%. TSMC maintains a rhythm of introducing new CoWoS technology annually and expects to further advance packaging size to 14x reticle by 2029.
However, as CoWoS capacity gradually catches up with demand, the advanced packaging bottleneck is shifting upstream to materials. He Jun warned that the increasing size and packaging complexity of AI accelerators are further driving up demand for ABF substrates, making them a critical constraint on advanced packaging expansion. Customers have already begun sourcing substrates from multiple suppliers to secure supply, but differences in thermal and mechanical performance among suppliers present new challenges for manufacturing consistency.

Upstream raw material suppliers, such as glass fabric producers, are also expected to gradually release new capacity starting in 2027, but the pace of expansion still struggles to fully keep up with the growth curve of AI chip demand.
He Jun emphasized that when packaging size exceeds 3x reticle, all component quality standards must reach automotive-grade or higher to keep failure rates within an acceptable range. This simultaneously raises the bar for material and manufacturing precision across the supply chain.
Regarding 3D packaging technology, He Jun pointed out that SoIC (System on Integrated Chips) has officially entered a high-growth phase. TSMC projects that SoIC capacity will grow at a compound annual growth rate (CAGR) exceeding 90% from 2022 to 2027, while CoWoS capacity will grow at a CAGR of over 80% during the same period. The 6-micron hybrid bonding pitch has already been introduced into mass production, with plans to further shrink it to 4.5 microns by 2029. This will be applied to direct A14 logic chip-on-A14 logic chip stacking, enabling vertical integration of logic chips using the most advanced process technology.
He Jun stated that SoIC, through true 3D IC and hybrid bonding technology, can deliver over 50 times the interconnect density of traditional interconnect solutions while improving power efficiency by approximately five times. This advantage is particularly critical for AI systems, because as computing demands surge, the overall power bottleneck is no longer just about chip computation itself; an increasing proportion comes from data movement between chips. By shortening the distance between chips and increasing die-to-die interconnect density, the power consumption required for data transmission can be effectively reduced.

He Jun vividly described his daily management of advanced wafer manufacturing as running "the world's largest online matchmaking service," where customers provide complex algorithms and ask TSMC to find the most suitable "soulmate" for each die. TSMC is leveraging chiplet "smart matching" technology to recombine performance-complementary bare dies, reducing waste caused by performance variations in individual chips and improving overall yield and effective output.
The function of the interposer is also evolving from simple interconnection to integration. In the future, it is expected to further carry active bridge chips, integrated voltage regulators, capacitors, and silicon photonics devices, forming a more complete system-level packaging platform.
Facing increasingly complex heterogeneous integration challenges, He Jun stressed the growing importance of "System Technology Co-Optimization" (STCO). As CoWoS packaging sizes continue to enlarge, development and validation at the component level alone are no longer sufficient to meet system requirements; chip, packaging, and overall system conditions must be considered together. To this end, TSMC is promoting parallel development among customers, suppliers, and fabs, releasing system specification guidelines six quarters before mass production, and requiring suppliers to station R&D teams near fabs to accelerate collaborative problem-solving on thermal, mechanical, and power issues.
He Jun concluded that the future development of AI advanced packaging is not just a technology race for a single company, but a collaborative effort across the entire ecosystem of chips, packaging, materials, equipment, and system integration. As CoWoS continues to scale up and SoIC hybrid bonding pitches continue to shrink, how to synchronize the evolution of each link through STCO, rapid validation, and continuous feedback will be the key to sustaining the advancement of AI advanced packaging.
