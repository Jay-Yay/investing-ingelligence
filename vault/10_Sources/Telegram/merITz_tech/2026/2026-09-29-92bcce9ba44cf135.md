### HBM Supply Constraints Persist, 2027 Price Outlook Revised Upward with Blended ASP Forecast to Rise 121% YoY, Says TrendForce

HBM Supply Constraints Persist, 2027 Price Outlook Revised Upward with Blended ASP Forecast to Rise 121% YoY, Says TrendForce
TrendForce’s latest research reveals that continued expansion in AI server demand is keeping HBM and conventional DRAM in competition for limited advanced-process and wafer capacity. Meanwhile, yields and output for next-generation products will take time to ramp, keeping memory supply tight through 2027.
Against a backdrop of continued memory price increases, suppliers have also taken a firmer stance on raising HBM prices in 2027. TrendForce has raised its 2027 HBM price outlook to reflect persistent supply constraints, a growing share of higher-priced HBM4, and the gradual ramp-up of HBM4e in 2H27. Blended ASP is now projected to jump 121% YoY.
Supply and cost constraints drive capacity adjustments, with 8-Hi emerging as a priority option
TrendForce reports that GPU and ASIC manufacturers are now considering reducing HBM capacity per device. This shift is driven not by waning end-market demand but by limited HBM supply and quickly increasing system costs.
Compared to earlier plans for 12-Hi HBM modules, 8-Hi configurations use fewer DRAM dies, lowering both the cost per stack and the overall GPU bill of materials. These configurations also enable the same HBM bit output to support more GPUs. Given the ongoing tight HBM supply, 8-Hi setups are expected to be a priority for evaluation by many GPU and ASIC vendors in 2027, balancing performance, supply, and cost.
However, reducing HBM capacity does not necessarily translate into a lower price per gigabit. Each HBM stack requires one base die, and this cost does not decline proportionally with the number of DRAM layers. An 8-Hi stack has less DRAM capacity over which to spread the base-die cost than a 12-Hi stack, making its per-Gb cost actually higher. TrendForce expects 8-Hi products to carry a roughly 10–20% price premium per Gb over 12-Hi products in 2027.
Overall, TrendForce expects the HBM market to remain supply-constrained in 2027. With both availability and rising costs shaping purchasing decisions, 8-Hi penetration is expected to increase as more GPU and ASIC vendors evaluate the configuration. However, its higher per-Gb cost relative to 12-Hi will remain an important consideration in specification negotiations and supply-chain planning. As a result, while adjustments to HBM capacity may help alleviate some GPU cost pressure in 2027, they are unlikely to reverse the broader trend of substantial HBM price increases.

[News] Samsung Sees HBM Taking Nearly 30% of Industry DRAM Capacity in 2027, Up From 20% Currently
AI-driven HBM demand is set to take up a growing share of global DRAM production capacity. According to Reuters, Samsung Electronics Executive Vice President Kim Taewoo said HBM is expected to account for nearly 30% of the DRAM industry’s total wafer capacity next year, up from about 20% currently. Kim noted that HBM and conventional DRAM compete for the same wafer capacity, meaning further HBM expansion could constrain conventional DRAM supply, the report adds.
At the company level, Samsung could also significantly expand its HBM capacity. According to Seoul Economic Daily, industry estimates suggest that Samsung’s average monthly HBM wafer input could rise nearly 40% from roughly 180,000 wafers this year to around 250,000 in 2027. The report adds that the HBM4 family could account for around 80% of Samsung’s HBM shipments next year, up from roughly 40% this year as HBM4E mass production ramps up.
The impact on conventional DRAM supply could be amplified by HBM’s greater wafer requirements. ijiwei notes that HBM consumes roughly three times as much wafer area as conventional DRAM. Put another way, the wafer area occupied by a single HBM die is roughly equivalent to that required for 3GB of DDR5. This means that as HBM’s share of DRAM capacity rises from 20% to 30%, conventional DRAM supply could be displaced at two to three times that pace.
