### Global Smartphone Revenue Defies Shipment Slump, Rising 3% YoY; Apple Records Highest-Ever Q3 Revenue

(https://buly.kr/450ov5X)

- Global smartphone revenue grew 3% YoY in Q3 2026 to $114 billion, as rising prices and premiumization offset a 19% YoY decline in shipments.
- The average selling price (ASP) surged 27% YoY to a record $442, driven by a higher contribution from premium devices and memory-driven price increases across OEMs.
- Apple recorded its highest-ever revenue for a third calendar quarter, capturing a record 50% share of global smartphone revenue in Q3 2026.
- Samsung ranked second with a 19% revenue share, with revenues up 14% YoY, while retaining its position as the largest smartphone brand by shipments with a 22% share. 
Beijing, Berlin, Buenos Aires, Fort Collins, Hong Kong, London, New Delhi, Seoul, Taipei, Tokyo – Nov 03, 2026 
 Global smartphone revenue grew 3% YoY in Q3 2026 to $114 billion, despite a steep 19% YoY decline in shipments, according to Counterpoint Research's latest Market Monitor Service. Higher average selling prices (ASPs) helped support revenue growth, offsetting the sharp decline in volumes. Premiumization, a stronger product mix and memory-driven price increases across segments pushed global ASP up 27% YoY to $442, its highest-ever level for a quarter.
Commenting on the market dynamics, Senior Analyst Shilpi Jain said, “The smartphone market is increasingly facing a trade-off between volumes and value. While higher ASPs have helped cushion the impact of declining shipments on revenue, this does not necessarily translate into profitability. As rising memory costs continue to squeeze margins, OEMs are raising prices, trimming marginal SKUs (stock keeping units) and prioritizing hero products and strategic markets. The entry-level segment is particularly vulnerable, as there is little room to absorb higher costs without compromising margins. For OEMs, the bigger challenge going forward will not just be defending shipment volumes but also protecting margins while keeping devices affordable.”

Apple recorded its highest-ever Q3 revenue with 17% YoY growth and captured a record 50% share of global smartphone revenue for the quarter, while its ASP also grew 19% YoY, supported by sustained demand for the iPhone 17 series and early traction for the iPhone 18 Pro series.
Commenting on Apple’s performance, Research Director Tarun Pathak said, “Apple remained the most resilient major brand in Q3 2026, with shipments nearly flat YoY. Apple has the pricing power to absorb rising memory costs and pass them on to consumers with minimal impact on demand. At the same time, ASP growth was supported by higher launch prices for the iPhone 18 Pro series, the absence of the base iPhone 18 from the launch and price increases across older models in September. Together, these factors helped Apple deliver strong revenue growth in an otherwise challenging market.”
Samsung ranked second with a 19% revenue share, with revenues up 14% YoY, while retaining its lead in shipments with a 22% share. Revenue growth was primarily driven by a 21% YoY rise in ASP to $367. ASP growth was supported by price increases across its volume-driven A series and stronger contributions from the Galaxy S26 Ultra and Z Fold8. Strong early demand for the Fold8 further supported its premium mix, while Samsung’s integrated supply chain helped cushion rising memory costs.
Xiaomi’s revenue remained flat YoY in Q3 2026. However, ASP grew by double digits, offsetting the volume contraction. This was driven by component cost pass-through and a clear portfolio shift away from the entry tier toward mid-range and upper-mid-range devices. ASP growth was further supported by a stronger contribution from higher-value models, with the Redmi Note 15 series and Xiaomi 17T featuring among the top revenue drivers across its core markets.
OPPO’s and vivo’s revenues declined 26% and 22% YoY, respectively, in Q3 2026, as shipment declines outweighed ASP growth. Both brands prioritized margins over volume, scaled back operations in non-core markets and focused on flagship and key models to strengthen brand positioning and drive revenue. OPPO recorded broader ASP gains across mid-range devices, supported by the OPPO A6 lineup, indicating an improving portfolio mix. vivo’s ASP growth remained concentrated in the upper-mid-range and premium segments, with the vivo S60 5G, V70 and X300 series contributing to its performance.
The revenue resilience seen so far is unlikely to continue into the next quarter. In Q4 2026, shipment declines are expected to outpace ASP growth, pushing global smartphone revenue into contraction. However, stronger ASPs in the first three quarters should keep full-year 2026 revenue higher YoY. In 2027, elevated memory costs will continue to pressure OEM margins and drive further price increases. Shipments are expected to decline again, though at a slower pace than in 2026, while higher ASPs should lift revenue growth above 2026 levels. Cost relief is expected only in 2028, when OEMs may reinvest savings in device upgrades rather than lower prices.

### [News] Apple Sees Holiday-Quarter Margins at 45%–46% as Memory Costs Soar; Inventory Jumps 154% Amid Stockpiling

(https://buly.kr/BIXt8Mv)

[News] Apple Sees Holiday-Quarter Margins at 45%–46% as Memory Costs Soar; Inventory Jumps 154% Amid Stockpiling
While memory giants cash in on soaring chip prices, Apple is feeling the squeeze. The issue took center stage at its November 2 earnings call, the first under new CEO John Ternus, who described the surge in memory prices as “unprecedented,” according to TechCrunch. As noted by Reuters, Apple has taken steps to secure supplies amid mounting cost pressures.
The impact is evident in Apple‘s swelling inventories. According to TechCrunch, citing the company’s earnings, total inventory jumped 31.1% QoQ and 154.3% YoY to $14.54 billion in fiscal 4Q26, as the iPhone maker continued stockpiling components, particularly memory chips.
Soaring Memory Costs Squeeze Apple’s Holiday-Quarter Margins
Rising memory chip prices are set to take a toll on Apple’s profitability in the crucial holiday quarter, Reuters reports, citing CEO John Ternus. According to the Financial Times, Apple expects fourth-quarter gross margins of 45%–46%, below Wall Street’s 47% forecast.
With memory costs set to climb further in the December quarter, Apple expects price hikes introduced earlier this year—including for Macs and iPads in late June and iPhones in September—to partly cushion the impact, according to TechCrunch.
Yet the margin squeeze comes as Apple eyes robust holiday-quarter sales, projecting revenue growth of 7%–10% YoY in 4Q26, ahead of analysts’ 6% estimate. CFO Kevan Parekh expects the period to mark Apple’s highest quarterly revenue on record, according to Seeking Alpha.
Apple’s latest results already showed solid momentum, with September-quarter revenue climbing 12% YoY from $102.5 billion a year earlier, according to CNBC. Notably, the growth came despite just eight days of iPhone 18 Pro sales and no contribution from the flagship iPhone Duo, which debuted after the quarter ended, the report adds.
The results also marked the first full quarter reflecting Apple’s broader price hikes, including a $100 increase for iPhones, introduced amid tightening memory and component supplies, CNBC explains. According to Reuters, Apple’s fourth-quarter iPhone revenue reached $57.58 billion, topping the $56.54 billion LSEG consensus estimate.
iPhone Duo Faces Supply Crunch
Beyond soaring memory costs, Apple’s iPhone Duo also came under scrutiny over prolonged supply shortages and reports of cracked displays. In an interview with Reuters, CEO John Ternus acknowledged that “a very small percentage” of units had been returned over screen issues, with Apple providing replacements.
Despite the concerns, demand continues to outpace production, with no clear timeline for when supplies might catch up. “Duo is constrained as of today,” Ternus said. “The ramp is going well… but at this point, we’re not able to predict when supply and demand will be in balance.”
Notably, Apple’s supply challenges go beyond the Duo. COO Sabih Khan warned that limited advanced-node chip availability is also constraining production across its iPhone, Mac and iPad lineups, according to Yahoo! Finance. As reported by Wccftech, Apple is marking a major silicon milestone with its new A20 Pro, a 2nm chip built by TSMC powering the iPhone 18 Pro, Pro Max and Duo.
TrendForce estimates that global foldable smartphone shipments will edge up just 0.5% in 2026. Apple’s first foldable iPhone is expected to ship approximately 5 million units, accounting for about 24.8% of the market in 2026. With the overall market remaining largely flat, Apple’s entry is poised to accelerate shifts in market share among competing brands.
