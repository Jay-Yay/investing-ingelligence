### Samsung Leveraging The Global DRAM Pricing Landscape With Its HBM4 Technology, Company Is Reportedly Squeezing Customers With A 3x Quote Compared To HBM3E

(https://vo.la/jwqIsdx)

HBM technology has reportedly been the driving force for Samsung’s foundry business recovery, and the technology giant is finding new ways to ensure that the next-generation memory retains its “cash cow” status. According to the latest figures, the company is said to charge three times the price for HBM4, and it appears that it already has some customers lined up.
At present, Samsung is negotiating a mid-to-high $4 price for HBM4, and it’s locking in supply for 2027
HBM3E is Samsung’s current flagship product, but HBM4 could soon eclipse its immediate predecessor in both revenue and supply. According to Maeil Business, the price for HBM4 is currently being negotiated at more than three times the figure of HBM3E. With the technology said to bring in industry-leading performance, Samsung intends to profit from this launch.
The price per gigabyte of HBM4 is said to be in the mid-to-high $4 range, whereas HBM3E’s estimated cost for the same capacity was $1.50. Perhaps the biggest advantage Samsung has, apart from its ability to secure Long-Term Agreements with customers for DRAM supply, is that HBM relies entirely on long-term annual contracts, with pricing and volumes negotiated well in advance.
After Samsung receives the initial sum, that’s when it formulates plans to commence mass production. After agreements are finalized regarding the finalization of HBM volumes with customers, Samsung is said to proceed to the next step this month, which is price negotiations.
From the looks of it, the increased bandwidth of HBM4 compared to HBM3E should be reason enough for Samsung’s customers to place heaps of orders, resulting in an increase in the ASP (Average Selling Price). In short, the company’s non-memory divisions, which were forecasted to shrink from 6.74 trillion won ($5.1 billion) in 2025 down to 3.92 trillion won ($2.95 billion) in 2026, could further decrease in 2027.

### [News] Micron Taiwan Investment Tops NT$1.6T as Four-Site Expansion Accelerates; Nanya Adds Pingtung Project

(https://vo.la/sNDSmHZ)

[News] Micron Taiwan Investment Tops NT$1.6T as Four-Site Expansion Accelerates; Nanya Adds Pingtung Project
Micron and Nanya Technology are stepping up investments in Taiwan as memory makers expand advanced DRAM and AI-related capacity. According to Economic Daily News, Micron Taiwan chairman Donghui Lu said the company is bringing its most advanced memory processes to Taiwan, with its second HBM advanced packaging facility in Taichung set to begin operations in October 2026 and further expansion underway in Tongluo, Taoyuan, and Tainan.
Meanwhile, Central News Agency reports that Taiwan’s National Science and Technology Council (NSTC) approved Nanya Technology’s plan to establish operations at the Pingtung Science Park, where the company plans to develop advanced 3D wafer packaging and testing using through-silicon via (TSV) and wafer-stacking technologies.
Nanya Technology has not disclosed the size of its Pingtung investment, though industry sources estimate that a new fab would require at least NT$100 billion, Economic Daily News notes. Micron, meanwhile, had invested more than NT$1.6 trillion in Taiwan as of June, with tens of billions more expected. Together with Winbond’s expansion in Kaohsiung, additional investments by the three memory makers are expected to approach NT$200 billion, as the report highlights.
Micron Expands Advanced DRAM and HBM Footprint in Taiwan
Among the three memory makers, Micron is pursuing a particularly broad expansion spanning front-end manufacturing and advanced packaging. According to Economic Daily News, its investment in Taiwan over the past four years has already matched the total from the previous roughly 25 years. Micron is also bringing its latest DRAM technologies to Taiwan, including its 1γ process, the company’s first to use EUV lithography, the report notes.
In Taichung, Micron’s largest manufacturing base in Taiwan, the company is expanding HBM back-end production. With existing advanced packaging space running short, Micron began converting a conventional facility into a second advanced packaging plant in 2025. Commercial Times adds that the facility, expected to come online in mid-October 2026, will primarily support HBM capacity expansion.
In Tongluo, Micron is expanding front-end capacity at the P5 fab acquired from PSMC earlier this year. More than 100 pieces of equipment have already moved in, while work on a second facility has begun, according to Economic Daily News. Central News Agency adds that Micron aims to begin the first phase of production at its expanding Tongluo site in the second half of 2027.
In Taoyuan, Fab 11E is being built for EUV equipment, with future High-NA EUV systems also factored into its design. The advanced-process facility is expected to come online around 2028, Economic Daily News notes.
In Tainan, Micron is accelerating equipment installation at a former AUO display panel facility that is being converted into a semiconductor site. Lu said an 11-meter-high warehouse has already been transformed into a three-story cleanroom, with equipment moving in since early September 2026. The site will primarily focus on front-end wafer testing, the Economic Daily News report notes.

### Elon Musk confirms discussions with TSMC about Terafab chipmaking collaboration — Intel is the only other named partner, Terafab to exclusively supply Tesla, SpaceX, and xAI | Tom's Hardware

(https://vo.la/1xTKGNw)

Elon Musk confirms discussions with TSMC about Terafab chipmaking collaboration — Intel is the only other named partner, Terafab to exclusively supply Tesla, SpaceX, and xAI
Musk confirms the talks, TSMC remains silent.
TSMC and Elon Musk's Terafab semiconductor production initiative are discussing a potential collaboration under which TSMC would build and operate a facility that will exclusively serve Elon Musk's Tesla, SpaceX, and xAI companies, according to Tim Culpan’s Culpium newsletter on Friday. Elon Musk confirmed the discussions early on Saturday, but did not elaborate.
"Just discussions, but something may come of it," Musk wrote in an X post, while TSMC has so far remained tight-lipped.
Under the arrangement Culpan considers most probable, TSMC would own and operate the new semiconductor production facility, while SpaceX/Terafab could invest in the venture, commit to buying a guaranteed volume of chips, or combine both approaches. However, the exact ownership structure remains under discussion. Another option would put SpaceX in control of the venture with the majority ownership; TSMC would invest less, but would still provide 'operational expertise' and process technologies. Perhaps, an interesting background detail from Culpium is that TSMC's rumored intentions to build a fab complex in Texas are tied to the foundry's effort to support Terafab.
The proposed 'Plan A' arrangement could resemble TSMC's JASM and ESMC joint ventures in Japan and Germany, where local partners provide capital and demand while TSMC contributes process technology and operates the fabs. The proposed 'Plan B' structure — under which SpaceX will hold the majority ownership of Terafab while TSMC would operate the facility and bring its own process technology — would be completely new for the world's No.1 contract chipmaker, though.
The development certainly raises questions about Intel's role in Terafab. For now, Intel is to supply its 1.4nm-class 14A process technology to Terafab, which would produce chips for Elon Musk's SpaceX, Tesla, and xAI. If TSMC becomes involved, then Intel's 14A could drop out of the game because TSMC will bring its own node.
Since nothing is official at this point, both 'Plan A' and 'Plan B' should be considered as speculations rather than the two options currently on the table, as there might be 'Plan C,' 'Plan D,' and even 'Plan E.'
TSMC is a leading advanced packaging supplier, so Musk's Terafab might be interested in making chips in Texas (presumably using Intel's 14A process technology) and then package them in Texas, which is where TSMC might come into play. Another thing to consider is that while Intel has a broad cross-patent license agreement with TSMC, Terafab does not, so producing chips using an Intel-developed node might infringe one of TSMC's patents, which might become a problem. To that end, Musk needs to keep TSMC close, perhaps to the point of buying into Terafab.

One thing to certainly keep in mind is that Elon Musk barely wants Terafab to become a foundry that competes against Intel Foundry, Samsung Foundry, or TSMC. What he needs is an uninterrupted and surplus supply of custom silicon to SpaceX, Tesla, and xAI to support their AI, automotive, and space vehicles. Given the rather broad strategic goal, Musk is probably very flexible when it comes to both tactics and strategy.
