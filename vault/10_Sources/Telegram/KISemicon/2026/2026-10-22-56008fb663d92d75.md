### NVIDIA To Connect 576 Rubin Ultra GPUs Using CPO To Create One Giant GPU With The "Highest Performance per Watt" on the Planet Next Year To Drive 10 Trillion-Parameter AI Models

(https://vo.la/pOHALZ6)

NVIDIA's Rubin Ultra GPUs will come in a massive 576-GPU rack-scale solution, all connected using next-gen CPO and NVLINK technologies.
NVIDIA Is Going All-In On CPO With Rubin Ultra GPUs, Scaling Racks To 576 Chips That Act As A Single GPU With The Highest Performance/Watt
Rubin Ultra is the platform that follows Rubin and takes everything a step further. While Vera Rubin "VR200" platforms are based around the Oberon NVL72 servers, Vera Rubin Ultra will be featured on the bigger Kyber rack, which is a fully liquid-cooled design.
As per the official specifications unveiled by NVIDIA during GTC, Rubin Ultra NVL576 will offer 15 Exaflops of FP4 (Inference) and 5 Exaflops of FP8 (training) performance. That's a 14x bump over Blackwell GB300 NVL72.
On the memory front, Rubin Ultra will feature up to 1 TB of HBM4E memory across a 4-reticle-sized GPU, which scales the memory by 8x, pushing out 4.6 PB/s bandwidth across 365 TB HBM per rack. Additionally, the platform will offer a next-gen 1.5 PB/s NVLink7 interconnect and a new ConnectX-9 Spectrum chip with 115.2 TB/s.
While there were rumors that NVIDIA might scale back its design for Rubin Ultra, the company told us in a follow-up that it “Continually optimizes compute, networking, memory” To deliver the best Performance & Efficiency to its customers.
During OCP 2026, NVIDIA's Ian Buck further disclosed some new information around Rubin Ultra. Ian states that all 576 GPUs inside the Kyber Rack will be connected with NVLink and work as one unit, making it the fastest GPU and the one with the highest performance per watt on the planet.
The Vera Rubin Ultra Compute Tray packs two Rubin Ultra GPUs and a single Vera CPU, all liquid-cooled. NVIDIA also showcases its NVLink 7 CPO Switch Tray for the first time.
Furthermore, the design will be based on the OCP standards. It leverages the same modular and easy plug design that has been introduced with Vera Rubin, dropping the installation time from hours to minutes.
We're already working on the next generation. Here's a sneak peek of Vera-Ribbon Ultra. This actually is not 72 GPUs in a single rack, but actually 576 GPUs that are all NVLink connector.
This is using our next generation NVLink CPO switch tray, and I have a picture of it here, along with our next generation Vera-Ribbon tray. You may notice some similarities here. This is all based on the OCP standards and it's actually going to be connecting all of those GPUs and all of those individual racks together with CPO networking to build one giant 576 GPUs all connected with NVLink.
There's little yellow fibers there connecting those optical engines. This will be the highest performance per watt NVLink giant GPU on the planet, and is designed to take us to 10 trillion parameter models to enjoy when we get there next year.
Ian Buck - NVIDIA Vice President and General Manager of Hyperscale and High-Performance Computing (HPC)
Equally significant is the fact that each rack will feature the next-generation NVLink CPO switch tray. NVIDIA's CEO, Jensen Huang, just announced during GTC Berlin that CPO racks are now in volume production, squashing reports of CPO delays. Next-generation NVIDIA optics solutions will be way faster, and Rubin Ultra will be the first rack-level system design to make full use of this technology, which is going to become the industry standard moving forward.
NVIDIA's Rubin Ultra NVL576 GPUs will be designed to tackle massive AI models, hovering in the 10 Trillion+ parameter range. Such deployments will require massive CapEx spending, & NVIDIA is leading from the front by delivering reference designs like the DSX Pod, which takes deployment times and shrinks them from months to just 14 days.
NVIDIA’s Rubin Ultra marks a decisive leap toward treating an entire 576-GPU liquid-cooled rack as one coherent superchip, powered by next-generation NVLink and CPO optics.
By fusing massive scale, extreme efficiency, and OCP-based modular design, it positions NVIDIA to deliver the performance-per-watt and rapid-deployment foundation required for 10-trillion-parameter models—while establishing CPO as the industry’s new interconnect standard.

### [Breaking] Google Reportedly Sticks With Intel EMIB-T Despite 35% Substrate Yields as HumuFish Schedule Tightens

(https://vo.la/vZRJeAE)

[Breaking] Google Reportedly Sticks With Intel EMIB-T Despite 35% Substrate Yields as HumuFish Schedule Tightens
Uncertainty is growing over potential delays to Google’s TPU v9. Industry sources point to the HumuFish variation as a key area of concern: reported yield problems with Intel’s EMIB-T substrates have raised the possibility of a switch to TSMC’s CoWoS-L. Supply chain checks, however, indicate that HumuFish remains “100%” committed to EMIB-T.
Yield Gap Raises Production Readiness Concerns
According to Common Wealth Magazine, EMIB-T’s architecture poses two major challenges for substrate suppliers. First, differences in thermal expansion between ABF substrates and embedded silicon bridges make precise alignment difficult during high-temperature processing. Second, NCF (Non-Conductive Film), applied beneath the bridges’ TSVs, can trap air bubbles during bonding, potentially weakening adhesion and further weighing on yields.
Against this backdrop, industry sources say that EMIB-T substrate yields were around 35% in 3Q26, well short of the 50% target set for year-end.
However, efforts are underway. While the reported bottleneck centers on substrate suppliers’ difficulties embedding TSV-equipped silicon bridge dies, industry sources note that Intel has adjusted its substrate electroplating process, while suppliers including Ibiden, Unimicron, and Shinko continue working to improve yields.
With HumuFish not slated for mass production until 1H28, the supply chain still has time to tackle the remaining challenges, according to industry sources.
TPU v9 Packaging Orders Split Between TSMC and Intel
According to industry sources, Google’s TPU v9 lineup is confirmed to include three variants: Broadcom-designed WhaleFish, and MediaTek-designed HumuFish and TriggerFish. WhaleFish is expected to adopt TSMC’s CoWoS-L, with mass production slated for 2H27. HumuFish, meanwhile, is set to enter mass production in 1H28, relying exclusively on Intel’s EMIB-T. TriggerFish is also planned to use EMIB-T, with production targeted for 2H28 or later.
TechNews, citing analyst Ming-Chi Kuo, further explains that TriggerFish is positioned as an upgraded TPU v9 with stronger inference capabilities, designed to address both CPU and memory bottlenecks. According to Kuo, TriggerFish features three key upgrades over HumuFish: 2–3 times the SRAM capacity, an additional simulation die, and a shift to HBM4E from HumuFish’s HBM4.
HumuFish’s Potential Timeline Slip
Amid recent market rumors of HumuFish’s delay, industry sources indicate that the tape-out of Google-designed Compute Die A, built on TSMC’s N2 process, has been pushed back to late October, while other dies remain on schedule.
Against this backdrop, industry sources suggest the delay is expected to push back trial production of the complete package. Commercial Times further notes that the Google TPU v9 project (HumuFish) involving MediaTek could see its tape-out pushed back to January 2027 due to design revisions, potentially delaying shipments until 1Q28.
Taken together, HumuFish’s 1H28 mass production target still gives the supply chain time to work through EMIB-T’s remaining issues, but a tape-out that slips to January 2027 could compress that cushion. How quickly substrate yields climb, and whether design milestones hold, will determine if HumuFish stays on schedule. The outcome will also be a key test of Intel’s ability to win large external customers for advanced packaging.
