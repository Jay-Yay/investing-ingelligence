### 삼성전자, 기흥 신규 R&D팹 '파운드리' 활용 추진…엔비디아 수요 선제 대응 - ZDNet korea

(https://vo.la/HEFz015)

삼성전자가 기흥에 구축 중인 차세대 반도체 연구개발(R&D) 단지 용도를 변경할 예정이다. 현재 해당 라인은 파운드리 생산능력 확대에 활용하기로 가닥을 잡은 것으로 14일 파악됐다. 당초 D램 양산용 라인으로도 투자가 논의됐으나 최근 계획이 수정됐다.
이곳에서 주력으로 노리는 양산품은 차세대 고대역폭메모리(HBM)용 2나노미터(nm) 베이스(로직) 다이다. 엔비디아 등 핵심 고객 주도로 HBM 공급이 확대될 전망인 만큼, 관련 생산능력을 미리 확보하려는 전략으로 풀이된다.
반도체 업계에 따르면 삼성전자는 기흥에 구축 중인 NRD-K 2라인 용도를 기존 R&D에서 파운드리 라인으로 활용하는 방안을 추진 중이다.
NRD-K는 삼성전자가 미래 반도체 기술을 선점하기 위해 건설 중인 최첨단 복합 연구개발단지다. 오는 2030년까지 약 20조원을 투입해 총 3개 라인을 조성하기로 했다. 이 중 첫 번째 라인은 지난 2024년 말 준공됐다.
올해부터 NRD-K 2라인 조성을 준비하고 있다. 기흥캠퍼스 내 옛 종합기술원 건물을 철거하고, 현재 부지 조성 등 기초공사 중이다.
다만 NRD-K 2라인 용도는 당초 계획에서 크게 변경될 확률이 높다. 최근 삼성전자는 해당 라인을 파운드리용 센드팹(Send-Fab)으로 활용하는 방안을 추진 중이다. 센드팹은 타 양산라인에서 웨이퍼를 받아 와 특정 공정을 대신 처리하는 보조 팹 개념이다.
목표로 검토 중인 주력 양산품은 엔비디아에 공급하는 HBM용 2나노 베이스 다이로 알려졌다. 베이스 다이는 HBM에서 여러 개의 D램을 수직으로 적층한 코어 다이 아래에 위치해, HBM과 그래픽처리장치(GPU) 등 시스템반도체 간 데이터 전송을 지원한다. HBM 성능을 좌우하는 요소 중 하나다.
HBM은 글로벌 빅테크의 공격적인 인공지능(AI) 인프라 투자에 따라 수요가 지속 확대될 것으로 관측된다. 특히 삼성전자는 향후 상용화될 커스텀 HBM과 HBM5(8세대 HBM)에 업계 최첨단 공정인 2나노 기술을 적용해, 경쟁사보다 성능 우위를 확보할 계획이다. 기흥 신규 팹을 활용해 관련 생산능력을 빠르게 확보하려는 것도 같은 이유로 보인다.
사안에 정통한 관계자는 "삼성전자가 2028년 하반기 개소를 목표로 NRD-K 2라인을 구축 중이고, 파운드리용 팹으로 활용하는 것으로 가닥을 잡았다"며 "NRD-K 2라인의 경우 팹 사이즈가 비교적 작은 편에 속하기 때문에, 센드팹 방식으로 최첨단 반도체 생산능력 확대에 기여할 것"이라고 설명했다.
물론 팹 오픈 시점이 2년가량 남은 만큼, NRD-K 2라인 용도가 또다시 변경될 수도 있다는 게 업계 시각이다.

### Samsung weighs moving legacy memory backend work to Vietnam to free up capacity for HBM

(https://vo.la/ejgkU8G)

Samsung Electronics may move part of the general-purpose DRAM and NAND packaging and testing now handled at its Cheonan and Onyang sites in South Korea to Vietnam, potentially freeing domestic backend capacity for high-bandwidth memory (HBM), according to DealSite.
The shift, described as under consideration rather than confirmed, would separate lower-value mature-memory backend work from Korean advanced-packaging capacity that Samsung increasingly wants to dedicate to HBM and other higher-value processes. The logic is direct: Vietnam absorbs conventional DRAM and NAND backend work, while Cheonan and Onyang gain production space and capacity for HBM.
Vietnam takes a clearer role
Samsung's Vietnam backend plant is not new. The company is building a US$1.5 billion semiconductor testing facility at Yen Binh Industrial Park in Thai Nguyen province, north of Hanoi, with operations targeted for November 2027. The plant is expected to focus on legacy memory, with planned maximum annual capacity of 153.3 billion Gb of DRAM and 255.6 billion Gb of NAND flash.
The potential production shift gives that site a clearer role inside Samsung's memory supply chain. Vietnam could take on general-purpose DRAM and NAND packaging and testing volume moved out of Korea, expanding legacy backend capacity offshore as HBM demand rises at home.
Samsung has also put the corporate structure in place. It established Samsung Vietnam Semiconductor (SVS) in the first quarter of 2026 and added the entity to its consolidated subsidiaries. Samsung's quarterly filing lists SVS as a semiconductor production subsidiary.
Korea pivots toward HBM
The potential offshore shift fits a broader reorganization of Samsung's Korean backend footprint that was already underway.
Samsung has been expanding HBM backend capacity at Onyang while redistributing roles between Cheonan and Onyang. Cheonan has been close to full, with retrofit work underway to remove older equipment and install new tools. Adding HBM capacity there has become more difficult, making Onyang expansion a practical route.
By July, the investment had taken clearer shape. Samsung laid out roughly KRW56 trillion for HBM-related facilities across Cheonan and Onyang. Onyang is set to house five HBM lines, while Cheonan will receive additional HBM equipment and production-line modernization.
The structural change at Onyang is the key point. The site, historically a conventional semiconductor backend base, is transitioning into an advanced HBM production hub.
As HBM takes a larger role at the two Korean sites, shifting some mature-memory backend work offshore would extend a capacity reallocation already underway.
A sharper division of labor
Taken together, the developments point toward a clearer geographic split in Samsung's memory backend. Vietnam would handle more mature-memory packaging and testing, while Korea concentrates limited production space and capital equipment on HBM and advanced packaging.
The shift could also help Samsung maintain legacy-memory output at a time when mature DRAM and NAND supplies remain tight as major producers dedicate more capacity to AI-related memory. A dedicated legacy backend operation in Vietnam would give Samsung another way to serve those markets without using as much Korean backend capacity.
None of this is settled. Samsung has not publicly confirmed a transfer of legacy production, and the scope, timing, and volume of any shift remain unreported. But the potential move fits a manufacturing shift already underway, with Cheonan and Onyang taking on a larger HBM role as Samsung builds conventional-memory backend capacity in Vietnam.

### Nvidia closing in on US$100B credit guarantee deal for OpenAI's Ohio data center

(https://vo.la/4gqy7Li)

Nvidia is approaching an agreement to provide credit guarantees of around US$100 billion for OpenAI's 10GW data center in Ohio, among the largest AI infrastructure projects ever, according to The Information. This is a significant reduction from earlier plans by Nvidia to guarantee US$250 billion for the project, although it would remain by far Nvidia's largest such commitment.
This credit support is expected to help finance the Ohio project's first construction phase, which may last two years and cost roughly US$250 billion in total. Afterwards, a second phase would complete the remaining buildout. The campus is estimated to cost at least US$500 billion overall, including chips, labor, power, and other materials, and is intended to house several data centers.
The site's power and data centers are being developed by SB Energy, a subsidiary of SoftBank Group and an investor in OpenAI. As part of Nvidia's credit support talks with OpenAI, the chipmaker is also considering investing US$3 billion in SB Energy, according to The Information. Nvidia may reportedly invest half when the parties reach an agreement over the Ohio project and the rest when SB Energy goes public. OpenAI and SoftBank each invested US$500 million in SB Energy in January 2026.
It was reported earlier that Nvidia was in discussions to provide credit guarantees worth US$250 billion for the project. This arrangement is meant to reassure lenders, allowing SB Energy to raise debt on better terms and OpenAI to secure a lease for the site. However, Nvidia's stock slid after reports of this figure emerged, and sources told the Wall Street Journal that the guarantee would be reduced to less than US$120 billion. Other sources told The Information that the original amount had not changed but would be split into two phases.
Nvidia becomes supplier and financier
Even though the new credit guarantee amount under discussion is scaled back from the original US$250 billion figure, it would still be far larger than Nvidia's previously reported guarantees, according to The Information. The Information reported in July that Nvidia was offering revenue guarantees to some cloud customers in exchange for a share of their revenue. The company also disclosed US$3.5 billion in lease guarantees earlier this year in exchange for rights to purchase shares in those companies.
While remaining the top supplier of AI chips, Nvidia's broader strategy appears to be aimed at expanding its customers' businesses with the expectation that this will lead to greater chip sales. Having invested US$30 billion in OpenAI, Nvidia may also view its large credit guarantee as a way to support that investment, potentially profit from the AI startup's possible IPO next year, and drive more chip purchases.
It has sought to spread exposure as AI leaders commit hundreds of billions of US dollars in building out compute, such as by partnering with six financial institutions this month to mobilize more than US$500 billion in third-party capital for AI infrastructure. This new model marks the evolution of computing infrastructure into an asset class that can be used to secure financing, with the proceeds then used to purchase more Nvidia chips. Although the risk is spread among other partners, Nvidia could nevertheless retain some exposure.
As for OpenAI, the Ohio project would be its first project as a direct tenant, rather than as a customer of another data center provider such as Microsoft or Oracle. The startup is seeking more direct control over its AI infrastructure and has raised its projected compute spending from US$600 billion to US$750 billion through 2030. This has sparked concerns that it is overspending before becoming profitable.
