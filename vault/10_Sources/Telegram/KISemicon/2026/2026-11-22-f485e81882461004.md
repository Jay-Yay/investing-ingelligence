### Samsung's Memory Production Reportedly Almost Fully Booked Through 2029, As Customers Seek Supply Deals Until 2031

(https://vo.la/vGvONuu)

Samsung Electronics' memory production capacity is reportedly almost entirely booked through 2029, as the ongoing AI infrastructure boom continues to strain global semiconductor supplies. Some customers are even seeking long-term agreements extending into 2031, potentially locking in memory supplies for another five years.
According to a new report from South Korea's Economy Tribune, citing industry sources, most of Samsung's memory manufacturing capacity between 2027 and 2029 has already been secured through contracts. Customers are reportedly requesting additional allocations beyond their existing commitments, highlighting the intense competition for available production capacity.
Samsung's Memory Supply Is Reportedly Almost Fully Booked Through 2029, With Long-Term Agreements Extending Into 2031
The latest report builds on Samsung's official third-quarter 2026 earnings announcement, during which the company confirmed that it had signed long-term supply agreements (LTAs) with 10 major customers. Negotiations with another 10 customers were also underway, with several reportedly approaching completion.
Samsung aims to allocate approximately 60% to 70% of its medium-to-long-term memory production capacity to multi-year contracts by 2030, giving the company greater revenue visibility while securing supplies for major technology customers.
Some customers are now reportedly pushing for agreements extending into 2031, potentially involving advance payments and contractual pricing mechanisms designed to protect both parties against future market volatility.
These arrangements are particularly significant given the growing demand for high-bandwidth memory (HBM), conventional DRAM, and enterprise storage products. Samsung previously confirmed that its 2027 HBM supply was already sold out, while industry sources now suggest that conventional DRAM wafer capacity for 2027 has also been effectively exhausted.
The situation is further complicated by HBM manufacturing requirements. Producing advanced HBM consumes substantial DRAM wafer resources, limiting how quickly manufacturers can expand conventional memory output even when production investments increase.
Industry sources cited by Economy Tribune estimate that leading memory manufacturers can currently satisfy only approximately 60% to 70% of customer demand, with supply constraints potentially intensifying in 2027.
AI Demand Could Keep DRAM Supplies Tight, Although NAND Market Forecasts Are Becoming Less Certain
The shortage is not exclusively affecting HBM. Conventional DRAM supplies are reportedly becoming increasingly constrained as manufacturers prioritize high-margin products for AI accelerators and data centers.
Enterprise NAND and SSD demand also remains substantial, according to the Korean report, with suppliers struggling to satisfy certain customer requirements.
However, the longer-term NAND outlook is considerably less certain. Research firm TrendForce recently projected that NAND contract prices could begin declining in the first quarter of 2027, citing reduced enterprise SSD orders from Chinese cloud providers and increasing manufacturing output.
This contrasts with the continued supply-tightness narrative and demonstrates that demand conditions may vary considerably between DRAM, HBM, and NAND markets.
Samsung itself has warned that additional capital expenditure will not immediately translate into higher memory output. New manufacturing facilities require years to become operational, while increasingly complex HBM products create additional limitations on effective production capacity.
For PC enthusiasts and consumers, the latest developments suggest that meaningful relief in DRAM availability may remain elusive, particularly if AI infrastructure investments continue at their current pace. However, long-term supply agreements do not necessarily guarantee permanently elevated memory prices, and changing demand conditions could eventually ease the pressure.
For now, Samsung's reported order commitments through 2029 provide another indication of how dramatically the AI boom has reshaped the memory semiconductor industry.

### SK하이닉스, 키옥시아 투자법인 전환사채 주식 전환…지분 14.1% 간접 보유 - 전자신문

(https://vo.la/VzFwG8R)

SK하이닉스가 일본 낸드플래시 기업 키옥시아에 투자하는 과정에서 인수한 특수목적법인(SPC)의 전환사채(CB)를 보통주로 전환한다.
SK하이닉스는 20일 공시를 통해 BCPE 판게아 케이맨2(SPC2)가 발행한 1290억엔 규모 CB를 보통주 12만9000주로 전환하고, 미국 베인캐피털 측이 보유한 SPC2 보통주 1001주를 1억엔에 추가 취득한다고 밝혔다.
앞서 SK하이닉스는 2018년 베인캐피털 주도 컨소시엄에 참여해 도시바메모리(현 키옥시아)에 총 3950억엔을 투자한 바 있다. 이 중 2660억엔은 BCPE 판게아 인터미디어트 홀딩스 케이맨(SPC1)에 출자하고, 나머지 1290억엔은 SPC2 CB 인수에 사용했다.
당시 SK하이닉스는 SPC1을 통해 투자 수익을 회수하고, SPC2 CB는 주식으로 전환할 수 있는 조건으로 투자에 참여했다.
이후 키옥시아가 2024년 도쿄증권거래소에 상장하면서 SPC1을 통한 투자 회수가 본격화됐다. 올해 6월 베인캐피털이 운용하는 SPC1의 키옥시아 보유 주식 전량 매각이 완료됐다.
SK하이닉스는 이번 CB 전환이 SPC1 펀드 청산 필요에 따라 SPC2에 남아 있는 투자구조를 정비하는 후속 절차라고 설명했다.
전환이 완료되면 SK하이닉스는 SPC2 보통주를 보유하게 되며, 이를 통해 키옥시아 지분 약 14.1%를 간접 보유하게 된다. 다만 SPC2가 보유한 키옥시아 주식 자체에는 변동이 없다.
회사는 이번 거래가 키옥시아 주식을 시장에서 매매하는 것이 아닌 기존 CB를 주식으로 전환하는 것으로, 보유 증권의 유형을 CB에서 보통주로 변경하는 것이라고 밝혔다. 키옥시아 지분을 처분해 투자 차익을 실현하는 거래와는 구분된다고 강조했다.
공시상 취득금액도 2018년 인수한 CB의 투자 원금을 기준으로 한 것으로, 베인캐피털 측 SPC2 잔여 지분 매입대금 1억엔 외에 신규 자금 투입은 없다는 것이다.
이번 전환으로 SPC2에 대한 주주권은 달라진다. 그러나 키옥시아에 대한 경영 참여에는 기존 투자약정과 관련 법령에 따른 제한이 계속 적용된다. 따라서 CB 전환이 키옥시아 경영 참여 확대를 의미하는 것은 아니라는 게 SK하이닉스 측 입장이다.
SK하이닉스 관계자는 “현재 키옥시아 지분의 추가 매입이나 매각 계획은 없으며, 앞으로도 키옥시아의 독립 경영을 존중해 나갈 것”이라고 밝혔다.

### NVIDIA Fuels Accelerated Computing at Microsoft With Vera Rubin Deployments at Azure, RTX Blackwell & Open Models For Windows PCs

(https://vo.la/5MRrcCG)

NVIDIA and Microsoft are bringing accelerated computing across Azure and Windows PCs, powered by next-gen infrastructure.
NVIDIA Vera Rubin NVL72 Now Available at Azure, While Nemotron Open AI Models & RTX Blackwell Power On-Premises Windows PCs
As accelerated compute and AI demand continue to grow, Microsoft is expanding its full-stack collaboration with NVIDIA, through which it will deploy next-gen Vera Rubin infrastructure on the Azure cloud.
NVIDIA Vera Rubin NVL72 Running in Azure
NVIDIA Vera Rubin NVL72 systems are running in Azure data centers, providing infrastructure designed for demanding reasoning, agentic applications, and large-scale model training and inference.
Microsoft and NVIDIA are also collaborating to extend confidential computing to bare-metal and rack-scale AI infrastructure. The work will help protect sensitive models and data while they are being processed, supporting organizations that need to work with proprietary or regulated information.
To help data centers get more performance from available power, NVIDIA and Microsoft are also collaborating on power efficiency using NVIDIA MaxLPS.
Azure’s Vera Rubin preview builds on NVIDIA and Microsoft’s codesign and performance optimization, underscored in the latest NVIDIA Blackwell-based MLPerf results. In MLPerf Training v6.1, Azure scaled to 10,240 NVIDIA GPUs with GB200 NVL72 systems on the DeepSeek-V3 model, the largest scale in the round. In MLPerf Inference v6.1, Azure’s NVIDIA GB300 NVL72 systems led cloud submissions on DeepSeek-R1 interactive throughput by over 10%, per Signal65.
Helping Enterprises Customize and Deploy Open Models
Enterprise AI agents need to reason through complex tasks, find relevant company information, and understand business documents. NVIDIA and Microsoft are expanding the models and services available through Microsoft Foundry to help developers bring these capabilities into business applications.
EY used Programmed Training in Microsoft Foundry to adapt NVIDIA Nemotron open models for classifying corporate purchasing records, improving accuracy on its evaluation dataset by 55% and lowering cost by 15x compared with proprietary frontier models. The work demonstrates how enterprises can customize open models for specialized business tasks.
The expanded NVIDIA Nemotron 3.5 lineup on Foundry Managed Compute supports different requirements across enterprise applications. Nemotron 3.5 Super is designed for long-running agents working across coding, research, and business workflows, while Lightning supports applications that need fast responses and tool use.
Bringing AI Closer to Enterprise Data
NVIDIA and Microsoft are expanding deployment options beyond the cloud, helping organizations run models and agents closer to their data while maintaining control over their infrastructure and operations.
Expanded support for Nemotron 3.5 in Foundry Local brings capabilities for reasoning, coding, and tool-using agents to Azure Local environments. Retrieval models help applications find evidence across text, document images, charts and tables.
Azure Local will also support NVIDIA RTX PRO 6000 Blackwell GPUs from hardware partners, giving organizations additional options for deploying AI on-premises.
Azure Local is expanding to NVIDIA Jetson Thor and Jetson Orin edge AI platforms to support industrial applications such as machine vision, robotics and autonomous operations. NVIDIA physical AI models, including Cosmos, are also being brought to Foundry Local.
On Windows PCs powered by NVIDIA RTX Spark, NVIDIA and Microsoft will showcase local large language model experiences at Ignite, demonstrating how developers can run AI applications directly on devices.
For organizations using Cloud PCs, the companies are bringing Direct Stream capabilities to GPU-accelerated Windows 365 Cloud PCs. These capabilities support demanding graphics applications with up to two 4K streams at 60 frames per second and low latency, even over challenging networks. A limited preview is open for sign-ups, with public preview planned for early 2027.
