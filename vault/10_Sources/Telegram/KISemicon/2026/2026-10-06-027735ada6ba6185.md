### [News] AMD’s Lisa Su Visits TSMC, AI Supply Chain Partners as Company Locks In Capacity 3–5 Years Ahead

(https://vo.la/wM9ZkYm)

[News] AMD’s Lisa Su Visits TSMC, AI Supply Chain Partners as Company Locks In Capacity 3–5 Years Ahead
AMD Chair and CEO Lisa Su made a brief visit to Taiwan as the company moves to expand supply for its growing AI business. According to Liberty Times, Su said AMD’s previously announced plan to invest more than US$10 billion in Taiwan’s industrial ecosystem remains fully on track, with further investments expected.
Su said AMD is increasing supply this year and plans to ramp it significantly further in 2027 as demand for AI-related products, including CPUs and GPUs, exceeds expectations. TSMC, backend manufacturing partners, and substrate suppliers are all increasing capacity, with Su’s visit aimed at ensuring the entire supply chain can expand accordingly, the report notes.
According to Wealth Magazine, the rapid growth is also changing how far ahead AMD plans with its partners. Su said capacity planning that once looked one to two years ahead now extends three, four, or even five years, with wafer fabrication, packaging, and substrates all needing to be ready at the same time.
Advanced packaging is a key part of those plans. Su said rising AI chip shipments are increasing demand for CoWoS, while AMD is also exploring other packaging approaches, including Fan-Out PLP with Taiwan’s supply chain. As Wealth Magazine notes, AMD does not view Fan-Out PLP as a replacement for CoWoS, with Su stressing that both CoWoS and other advanced packaging technologies will remain important.
Next-Generation AI Platforms Drive Capacity Expansion
The capacity push comes as AMD ramps its next-generation AI platforms. As noted by Economic Daily News, AMD began production of its MI450-series AI platform in the second half of 2026, using TSMC’s 2nm node and CoWoS. The MI450 series entered production at TSMC in the third quarter, with capacity expected to ramp gradually by the end of 2026 before higher-volume shipments begin in 2027. According to the report, the succeeding MI455 series is scheduled to enter production in the first half of 2027, with preparations for its ramp now in focus.
Asked whether TSMC has sufficient capacity to meet AMD’s needs, Su said the company is working closely with all of its partners, including TSMC, TechNews notes. She added that TSMC’s new fab in Arizona is progressing very well and that AMD looks forward to seeing more capacity expand globally to meet strong market demand.
Taiwan Supply Chain Supports AMD’s AI Server Push
The ramp-up is also expected to drive greater demand across AMD’s Taiwan AI server supply chain. Foxconn is among the Taiwanese suppliers positioned to benefit from AMD’s expansion. Economic Daily News notes that the company is one of AMD’s major AI server suppliers, with its involvement spanning substrates and thermal modules to complete AI server assembly for cloud service providers including Microsoft and Oracle. The group also supplies high-speed switches and optical transmission equipment alongside AMD-based servers.
AMD Looks to Secure Memory Supply in South Korea
AMD is also working closely with memory chipmakers to secure sufficient supplies, Su said ahead of her visit to South Korea, home to Samsung and SK hynix, Reuters notes. Whether Su will hold another high-level meeting with Samsung Electronics executives is drawing attention. As highlighted by Seoul Economic Daily, Samsung is set to supply sixth-generation HBM4 for AMD’s next-generation GPUs, while further talks may include discussions on expanding their partnership beyond memory into areas such as mobile devices and AI PCs. Attention is also on whether Su will meet with executives from SK hynix and LG Electronics, the report adds.

### [News] Qualcomm, Apple, MediaTek Battle It Out on TSMC 2nm: Inside Their Flagship AI Strategies

(https://vo.la/oSeTTi3)

[News] Qualcomm, Apple, MediaTek Battle It Out on TSMC 2nm: Inside Their Flagship AI Strategies
The flagship mobile chip race is entering the 2nm era, with on-device AI emerging as a key battleground. According to ijiwei, Qualcomm, Apple, and MediaTek all unveiled TSMC-made 2nm processors in September 2026, marking the industry’s shift to GAA nanosheet transistors. While all three are putting AI at the center of their latest chips, their approaches differ significantly, from larger on-device models and always-on AI agents to higher memory bandwidth and improved thermal design.
Notably, the simultaneous shift to 2nm is also increasing demand for TSMC’s manufacturing capacity. According to Economic Daily News, Apple, NVIDIA, AMD, Qualcomm, and MediaTek have reportedly increased their reservations for TSMC’s 2nm family by 10% to 20%, prompting the foundry to accelerate its capacity expansion. Industry sources cited by the report expect TSMC’s 2nm monthly capacity to reach around 120,000 wafers by the end of 2026, above previous market estimates of 90,000 to 100,000.
Qualcomm Pushes Larger AI Models On-Device
Qualcomm’s Snapdragon 8 Elite Gen 6 and higher-end Snapdragon 8 Elite Extreme Gen 6 are both built on a 2nm process, with on-device AI at the center of the upgrades. According to Qualcomm, the redesigned Hexagon NPU and Sensing Hub are designed to support more personalized AI agents that can learn and remember user preferences. The Extreme Gen 6 goes further, supporting mixture-of-experts (MoE) models with more than 30 billion parameters directly on-device, Qualcomm notes. The two chips will first appear in flagship devices from brands including Honor, iQOO, Motorola, OnePlus, OPPO, Redmi, RedMagic, vivo, and Xiaomi.
Apple Focuses on AI Computing, Memory and Cooling
Apple is taking a different approach with the 2nm A20 Pro, emphasizing AI compute, memory bandwidth, and thermal performance, ijiwei notes. According to Apple, its new 32-core Dual Neural Engine delivers twice the AI processing power of the A19 Pro, while memory bandwidth increases by 50%. Apple has also introduced new M-series-inspired packaging that places the silicon die and memory side by side, moving memory out of the chip’s thermal path and allowing the A20 Pro to connect directly to a next-generation vapor chamber. Apple says the design improves heat dissipation and sustained performance, an increasingly important consideration for demanding on-device AI workloads. The A20 Pro powers the iPhone 18 Pro lineup as well as the iPhone Duo.
MediaTek Targets Always-On AI With Dual NPUs
MediaTek’s Dimensity 9600 Pro is also built on a 2nm process, but takes a dual-NPU approach to on-device AI. According to MediaTek, its second-generation high-efficiency NPU cuts power consumption by 40% for always-on AI, while the higher-performance NPU 1090 delivers 51% faster LLM prefill performance and 55% more token generation per watt. The Dimensity 9600 Pro supports on-device models with up to 30 billion parameters, with the dual-NPU design aimed at balancing demanding AI workloads with continuously running AI agents.
MediaTek also introduced the Dimensity 9600M for a broader range of high-end smartphones. Unlike the 9600 Pro, however, the 9600M uses TSMC’s 3nm process, according to Reuters.
