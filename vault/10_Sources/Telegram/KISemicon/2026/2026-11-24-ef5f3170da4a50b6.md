### [News] SK hynix’s Indiana Plant Clears Nearly Two-Year Rezoning Dispute Ahead of 2029 Production

[News] SK hynix’s Indiana Plant Clears Nearly Two-Year Rezoning Dispute Ahead of 2029 Production
As SK hynix presses ahead with its U.S. expansion, a key legal hurdle has been cleared. According to Korea Credit News, the West Lafayette City Council in Indiana has approved a settlement with local residents, ending a nearly two-year dispute over the industrial rezoning of the company’s advanced semiconductor packaging plant site.
The report explains that the dispute began after SK hynix announced plans to invest $3.87 billion (approximately KRW 5.4 trillion) in a semiconductor packaging plant on a 90-acre site near Purdue University. While originally zoned for residential use, the city council, in May 2025, voted 6–3 to rezone the property for heavy industrial use, prompting three nearby residents to file a lawsuit against the city.
As highlighted by Korea Credit News and The Tracker, the city council subsequently voted to bring the lawsuit to a close on November 19, effectively canceling the trial originally scheduled for December 1.
The decision, per The Tracker, could help SK hynix preserve a critical window for investment and capacity expansion, as semiconductor manufacturing requires years of lead time, from initial investment decisions and plant construction to equipment installation and the eventual start of mass production.
Notably, the agreement includes a clause barring both sides from making disparaging remarks related to the lawsuit for the next two years, according to Korea Credit News.
SK hynix’s Current Progress in Indiana
The memory giant has already reached a key milestone for U.S. manufacturing earlier in August. According to SK hynix’s press release, the company marked the start of construction on its Indiana fab with an August 27 groundbreaking ceremony at Purdue University’s Holloway Gymnasium in West Lafayette.
As reported by Reuters, SK hynix plans to begin mass production of next-generation HBM4E chips at its Indiana facility in the third quarter of 2029, with CEO Kwak Noh-Jung expecting the plant to eventually achieve an annual production capacity of hundreds of thousands of wafers.
SK hynix expects its Indiana facility, located at Purdue Research P, Reuters reports, adding that cleanroom operations are slated to begin in the second half of 2028.

### [News] TSMC Reportedly Eyes US$22.6B Fab for 2nm and Beyond in Brazos County, Texas, Targeting 2031 Completion

(https://vo.la/VMhmMaS)

[News] TSMC Reportedly Eyes US$22.6B Fab for 2nm and Beyond in Brazos County, Texas, Targeting 2031 Completion
TSMC’s potential expansion into Texas is taking more concrete shape after Chairman and CEO C.C. Wei confirmed in October that the company had sent teams to evaluate manufacturing opportunities in the state. According to local broadcaster KBTX, TSMC has now filed a tax incentive application for a proposed US$22.6 billion semiconductor fab in Brazos County, Texas. The filing with the Texas Comptroller’s Office indicates that construction is expected to begin in 2027 and be completed in 2031.
As noted by Texas broadcaster KXXV, citing Brazos County, the proposed facility would manufacture logic chips using 2nm or more advanced process for AI, high-performance computing (HPC), and mobile applications. The project is expected to create at least 2,099 direct jobs, with an average annual salary of US$133,000.
Citing the filing, KBTX reports that TSMC proposes building at least one fab on more than 3,200 acres of land. While local officials say the parcels are under contract, county records show that the land has yet to be purchased, and TSMC’s filing indicates that the agreements are not yet binding.
The chipmaker is seeking incentives under Texas’ Jobs, Energy, Technology and Innovation (JETI) program, which offers a 10-year school district property tax break in exchange for significant capital investment and local job creation, the report adds.
TSMC’s Texas Investment Awaits Final Decision
Despite the latest filing, Brazos County has yet to secure the investment, as the project remains subject to government approvals and TSMC’s final investment decision, KXXV notes. As the proposal is evaluated, Brazos County is coordinating with the cities of College Station and Bryan, College Station ISD, Texas A&M University, and the Greater Brazos Partnership. The county also plans to hold a public town hall to address residents’ questions and launch a dedicated webpage with further project details and information on upcoming public meetings, according to a county news release published by local radio station WTAW.
The proposed Brazos County facility adds another potential location to TSMC’s U.S. expansion plans, following earlier reports identifying Sherman in North Texas as a possible site. South Korea’s ETNews reported in early November that Sherman had entered site selection talks with TSMC. Dallas-based broadcaster WFAA, citing the Dallas Business Journal, reported that Texas state development documents outlined a potential US$45 billion investment by an unnamed semiconductor manufacturer to build two fabs, create approximately 5,000 jobs over six years, and begin production by late 2029. However, the documents did not identify TSMC as the investor.
