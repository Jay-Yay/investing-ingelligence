### [News] TSMC Reportedly Eyes 20% CoWoS Price Hike, Skips Equipment Price Renegotiations Amid AI Demand Surge

(https://vo.la/Os2AfaW)

[News] TSMC Reportedly Eyes 20% CoWoS Price Hike, Skips Equipment Price Renegotiations Amid AI Demand Surge
As increasingly large AI accelerators strain advanced packaging capacity, TSMC’s CoWoS supply remains tight. According to supply chain sources cited by Commercial Times, the foundry giant is reportedly weighing price hikes of up to 20%, with some packaging lead times already stretching.
The pricing pressure comes as advanced packaging demand expands beyond AI GPUs to server CPUs and custom ASICs, adding to a growing backlog of unfilled orders. Against this backdrop, Commercial Times reports that foundries and outsourced semiconductor assembly and test (OSAT) providers are considering another round of CoWoS price increases of 10%–20%.
Adding to the capacity squeeze, longer AI chip testing times are emerging as another bottleneck. Commercial Times notes that extended testing cycles are further straining packaging and testing capacity. Underscoring the challenge, Seoul Economic Daily, citing Nomura Securities, reports that NVIDIA Blackwell’s final testing takes an estimated four times as long as Hopper’s, while Rubin could require seven times as long.
The capacity crunch is also rippling through the equipment supply chain. According to Commercial Times, equipment lead times have lengthened to six to eight months, up from three to six months, as customers prioritize securing deliveries over negotiating prices.
Even TSMC appears to be shifting its procurement approach amid the equipment supply squeeze. According to Commercial Times, the foundry giant, which typically negotiates price cuts or additional features at no extra cost, reportedly placed equipment orders at existing prices this year without seeking discounts or upgrades.
Notably, TSMC is not only seeking price increase for CoWoS. According to Liberty Times, the company has finalized plans to raise wafer-out prices by 3%–6% starting January 2027, depending on the process node, with advanced nodes such as 2nm and 3nm seeing the steepest increases.
TSMC’s CoWoS Expansion in Focus
Amid high demand for CoWoS, TSMC continues to expand its advanced packaging capacity, with analysts cited by Commercial Times estimating monthly CoWoS capacity will exceed 130,000 wafers in 2026 and target 200,000 in 2027.
Goldman Sachs, meanwhile, projects TSMC’s CoWoS capacity to reach 1.275 million wafers in 2026, or roughly 106,000 per month, before surging 114% YoY to 2.73 million in 2027 (227,500 monthly). Capacity is then expected to climb another 27% to 3.48 million wafers in 2028, equivalent to nearly 290,000 per month, Goldman Sachs adds.
Beyond capacity expansion, TSMC is also pushing the boundaries of advanced packaging to accommodate increasingly large AI chips. At its latest earnings call, Chairman and CEO C.C. Wei highlighted strong yields for its 5x reticle-size packaging, already in mass production, while confirming development of 9x and 14x solutions. The company is also advancing CoPoS to support larger packages at lower costs, with Commercial Times pointing to a 2028 timeline for 14x packaging.

### China’s DRAM Self-Sufficiency To Remain A Distant Dream, As Local Giants Seek 10-Year LTAs From Samsung And SK hynix; CXMT & YMTC Lagging In Key Areas

(https://vo.la/7PvFkoV)

Chinese memory makers CXMT and YMTC have a mountain to climb as far as meeting demand goes because there are still local technology giants like Alibaba, Tencent, and ByteDance whose DRAM shipment requirements aren’t being fulfilled. Seeing as how their home-based suppliers have their hands full, it falls upon Samsung Electronics and SK hynix to make up for this production gap. According to the latest report, these behemoths are setting up lengthy Long-Term Agreements (LTAs) spanning 10 years, which is well beyond the 3-5-year period, meaning that the contracts will expire in 2035.
ByteDance, Alibaba, and Tencent are likely showing preference to overseas hardware due to technological superiority, as the same Chinese firms chose NVIDIA’s RTX PRO 5500 over Huawei’s Ascend for AI inference
The items being requested are server DDR and LPDDR memory, according to Maeil Business Newspaper. DDR serves as the main component that lets a server’s CPU read and write data quickly, while LPDDR, usually found in smartphones, is increasingly in demand for AI servers because of its power efficiency. With supply already tight, server makers are reportedly rushing to secure LPDDR in particular.
Samsung and SK hynix are reportedly being given priority because CXMT and YMTC are assessed as lacking the product competitiveness and mass production scale to supply high-performance server memory, which makes it difficult for Chinese cloud providers to source the volumes they need at home. CXMT, in particular, was rejecting offers from Apple because it’s said that the company wished to cater to local customers first to achieve China’s DRAM self-sufficiency.
Based on the latest report, that goal has fallen short by a considerable margin, but what’s interesting is that CXMT was also mentioned as lacking product competitiveness. The manufacturer was seemingly the first to supply LPDDR6 RAM for Xiaomi’s in-house XRING O3 SoC, albeit in limited quantity, indicating that CXMT’s technology prowess shouldn’t be ignored just because it lacks Samsung’s or SK hynix’s current production capabilities.
Still, ByteDance, Alibaba, and Tencent would much more likely prefer getting their hands on timely shipments, and with U.S. hyperscalers already having locked in their volumes, Chinese buyers seem keen not to fall behind. Even if CXMT has achieved bragging rights, such as shipping commercial LPDDR6 RAM, its long-term contracts depend on how much supply it can allocate to each customer.
In this regard, Samsung and SK hynix hold immense superiority, with the former also reportedly setting up a 25 percent premium on HBM for its AI data center customers since it can fulfill massive orders for these technology titans. It’s worth noting that the report relies on industry sources, and the 10-year demand is described as coming from “some clients,” not all of them, so it’s important to treat these details with a pinch of salt.
We should also note that there’s no evidence that Samsung or SK hynix has agreed to such terms, and any deals involving Chinese customers would need to navigate export controls and geopolitical considerations, which the report does not address. Still, both DRAM manufacturers remain central to the AI infrastructure, especially in a market where China is investing heavily to build its own alternatives.
It could also be said that ByteDance, Alibaba, and Tencent might display a preference towards overseas manufacturers’ parts simply because of their unmatched quality. After all, all three reportedly placed orders for more than 3 million RTX PRO 5500 GPUs, despite reportedly being informed by NVIDIA that it couldn’t keep up with demand. Huawei’s Ascend 950PR also competes with these chips, but local Chinese giants have a different chain of thought, which is something to keep in mind considering the quality of CXMT and YMTC memory.

### Elon Musk Wanted 30,000 Of NVIDIA's Bleeding Edge AI GPU Systems But Is Getting Just 20,000, Still Enough To Be 2027's Biggest Buyer, Says Analyst Jeff Pu

(https://vo.la/sVcHERv)

Elon Musk's SpaceX will be a major buyer of NVIDIA's latest Vera Rubin AI GPUs in 2027, says analyst Jeff Pu of Korean financial firm GF Securities. SpaceX, which has positioned itself as a cloud computing services provider, will account for roughly one-third of the 2027 VR200 GPU demand, as other service providers such as CoreWeave account for a much smaller share of the total supply of 96,000 NVL72 racks.
SpaceX To Account For Nearly 21% of 2027 Expected NVIDIA Vera Rubin NVL72 AI GPU Rack Consumption, Says Analyst
Today's report comes after Musk made a series of strong remarks for the neocloud sector amidst concerns about high borrowing. While traditional software companies relied on data center infrastructure composed of CPUs, artificial intelligence providers often rely on specialized data centers called neoclouds.
Over the weekend, not only did Musk outline that SpaceX had raised $40 billion in debt to finance NVIDIA's pricey GPUs but added that his firm was able to do so because of its high credit rating. He added that debt markets were unfavorable to firms which lacked an alternative revenue stream and opined that "200+ neoclouds are going to see guppy-level death and consolidation. Some will make it through, but not many."
Now, Pu's report points toward where some of this money might be being spent. The analyst outlines that NVIDIA's Vera Rubin NVL72 rack system's total addressable market (TAM) should sit at 73,000 by 2026 end and at 96,000 at 2027 end. Diving into the 2027 expectations for the chip, the analyst outlines that SpaceX is expected to be the largest buyer of the rack systems.
Specifically, Pu outlines that while SpaceX had demanded 30,000 NVL72 racks from NVIDIA, it is being allocated 20,000 racks instead. Cumulatively, the analyst adds that the six major neoclouds will account for 35% of the industry's demand in 2027 by procuring 34,000 out of the 96,000 racks. As for the final assembly, the GF Securities analyst believes that Taiwan's Foxconn has secured as much as 45% of SpaceX's data center orders.
While NVIDIA manufactures the Vera Rubin GPUs, they are assembled into racks such as the NVL72 by contract manufacturers including Foxconn. A single NVL72 rack has been reported to be quite pricey, with estimates often crossing into millions of dollars on the back of record prices in a supply-constrained memory chip market.
