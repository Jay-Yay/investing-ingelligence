### Samsung Foundry Reaches Full Capacity in Texas, Accelerating 2nm Push - Businesskorea

(https://www.businesskorea.co.kr/news/articleView.html?idxno=276433)

Tesla, Broadcom, and Arm Orders Fill Taylor Fab Before Mass Production Begins
The Samsung Electronics foundry (semiconductor contract manufacturing) division is launching a massive counterattack in the global foundry market dominated by Taiwan’s TSMC, amid being driven by large-scale orders from U.S. big techs such as Tesla and Broadcom. The Taylor foundry fab (plant) in Texas, U.S., which will begin trial operations as early as the end of this month, has already reached ‘full capacity’ with its scheduled production volume fully booked, and it has been confirmed that the introduction of equipment from domestic partners to be brought into the second plant, which will begin construction at the end of the year, is also underway.
This is interpreted as a strategic move to secure mass production capabilities on par with TSMC in the 2-nanometer (nm, 1 nm = 1 billionth of a meter) sector, a cutting-edge process that will determine the leadership of the global foundry market next year, and to preempt big tech’s ‘de-TSMC’ demand.
According to the semiconductor industry on Sept. 8, Samsung Electronics’ Taylor fab in the U.S. has virtually reached a full capacity state even before operation as 2nm orders continue to roll in, including Tesla’s next-generation artificial intelligence (AI) chip ‘AI5,’ Broadcom’s next-generation communication semiconductor, and the UK’s Arm’s AI chip for smart devices. The 2nm yield (the ratio of non-defective products), which was at the 60% level early this year, is known to have recently reached the 80% range capable of mass production, thanks to the yield stabilization of existing advanced processes such as 4nm and 5nm.
The Taylor fab, which Samsung Electronics built by investing $37 billion since 2022, will go through trial operations early next month and begin mass production next year. The initial production capacity is 50,000 wafers per month based on wafer input, which is close to Taiwan’s TSMC (80,000 wafers). Previously, Samsung Electronics dispatched personnel in charge of process, equipment, and yield to the Taylor fab in June, followed by the start of equipment installation in July.
As the Taylor fab, the core base of Samsung Foundry, enters a full capacity state, the performance of the foundry division, which is an ‘aching weakness’ of the semiconductor (DS) unit, is also rising rapidly. During a recent earnings release, Samsung Electronics forecasted that the volume of 2nm orders this year will more than double compared to last year. Nvidia’s inference-dedicated chip ‘Groq3’ also entered mass production starting last month. Groq3, based on the 4nm process, is a system that bundles 256 language processing units (LPUs) into a rack scale, and it takes on the role of expanding the inference performance of the next-generation AI platform ‘Vera Rubin.’ The fact that Samsung Electronics recently raised the price of its 4nm foundry by up to 15% also affected the performance increase.
Preparations for the Taylor Plant 2, which is scheduled to break ground at the end of this year with the goal of mass production in 2030, are also accelerating. It is understood that Samsung Electronics recently requested major partners to obtain safety certification in advance for the semiconductor production equipment to be introduced to Plant 2. It is also reported that some partners are pushing forward with a plan to dispatch dozens of personnel to the site for the construction of the Taylor Plant 2.
The fierce battle for dominance is also heating up. US Intel issued $20 billion worth of new shares last month and is expanding investments in the 2nm-class ‘18A’ process and the next-generation 1.4nm-class ‘14A.’ Rapidus, the symbol of Japan’s semiconductor revival, also completed a 2nm foundry plant in Hokkaido last month and entered the wafer trial input stage.
