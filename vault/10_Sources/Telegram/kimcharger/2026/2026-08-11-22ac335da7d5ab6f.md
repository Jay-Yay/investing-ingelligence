### MLCC Supply-Demand Gap Widens Sharply as Clients Pay 2-3x Premiums, Lead Times Stretch to 16 Months — BigGo Finance

(https://finance.biggo.com/news/75572009-ff5b-4a49-b737-3c0b1556cd4b)

MLCC Supply-Demand Gap Widens Sharply as Clients Pay 2-3x Premiums, Lead Times Stretch to 16 Months
The global multilayer ceramic capacitor (MLCC) market is undergoing a new supply crisis. Passive component leader Yageo confirmed to Wall Street CN that MLCC demand is "indeed very strong." Driven by the AI server and data center buildout wave, an increasing number of clients, seeking to avoid material shortages, are willing to pay two to three times the original price to compete for supply. Delivery lead times have significantly extended from normal levels to 12-16 months, creating a tense "highest bidder wins" atmosphere in the market.
This extremely tight supply-demand situation quickly transmitted to capital markets. On August 11, China's A-share MLCC concept stocks led gains collectively. As of press time, industry leader Fenghua Advanced Technology (000636.SZ) surged at the open, rising 9.37% to 67.48 yuan, pushing its total market capitalization to 78 billion yuan (approximately $11.6 billion). Its single-day trading volume reached nearly 7.5 billion yuan (approximately $1.1 billion), ranking fifth across China's A-share market. Shuangxing New Materials (002585.SZ), Jiemei Technology (002859.SZ), and Guofeng New Materials (000859.SZ) all hit their daily trading limits, while Three-Circle Group (300408.SZ) rose over 5%. As early as the afternoon of August 10, this sector had already shown a volatile rebound, with Yunchou Technology and Longxin Intelligent surging over 10%, signaling clearly warming market expectations for related supply chain beneficiaries.

AI Demand Ignites Long-Term Contract Lock-in Wave
The core driver behind this MLCC supply shortage is the explosion of artificial intelligence applications. According to Wall Street CN, Yageo noted that market heat is continuously rising, from capacity utilization rates and new client projects to demand for long-term agreements. Notably, an increasing number of AI-related clients are proactively requesting to lock in capacity supply for the next six months to several years to mitigate future supply risks.
Yageo had already signaled a strong supply-demand inflection during an earlier earnings call. The company expects standard product capacity utilization to rise from approximately 80% in the second quarter to over 90% this quarter, while specialty products will remain above 90%, with overall utilization moving toward full capacity.
According to Taiwan's Economic Daily News, major MLCC manufacturers currently have full order books, with existing capacity prioritized for large clients and long-term partners. The rapid ramp-up of high-end applications such as AI servers and data centers is further squeezing the supply available to small and medium-sized clients. Industry analysis suggests that once smaller clients face low inventory and fear that material shortages will impact end-product shipments, they have no choice but to bid higher prices for limited supply, with some rush orders priced at two to three times the original cost.
To meet surging client demand, Yageo is simultaneously increasing capacity across its production bases in Kaohsiung (Taiwan), Suzhou (mainland China), Vietnam, and Mexico. As related equipment arrives progressively, new capacity will be released quarter by quarter.
Note: The generational shift in AI hardware is driving an order-of-magnitude jump in per-unit MLCC usage. According to Taiwan-based research firm TrendForce, AMD's MI450 platform, during the validation phase, completely replaced original aluminum electrolytic capacitors and tantalum capacitors with MLCCs. The per-board usage of 47μF/2.5V X6S 0402 specification MLCCs skyrocketed from 1,440 units to 10,544 units, an increase of 632%. Taiwan's China Times also cited industry sources analyzing that NVIDIA's latest-generation AI rack requires up to 440,000 MLCCs, compared to approximately 2,200 units in the previous generation — an 8 to 10-fold jump in usage. This is the fundamental reason why utilization rates at major manufacturers like Yageo are rapidly reaching full capacity while lead times simultaneously extend.
Japanese Majors Also Bullish, High-End Products Continue Expanding
Strong demand is not unique to Yageo; leading global MLCC manufacturers are all sending positive signals. Global MLCC leader Murata Manufacturing of Japan recently significantly raised its annual profit forecast, citing the data center buildout boom as a key driver.
Taiyo Yuden, the world's third-largest MLCC manufacturer, also stated directly that AI server demand has exceeded original expectations, with the market accelerating toward higher-capacitance products. This is driving continued increases in MLCC usage per device, providing further support for overall market conditions.

Market Outlook and Potential Risks
This wave of MLCC price hikes and shortages inevitably draws market comparisons to the dramatic cyclical volatility previously seen in the memory chip industry. While the certainty of AI demand provides solid growth momentum for the passive component sector, whether clients' "premium bidding" and "long-term contract lock-in" behavior will lead to double ordering and excessive inventory buildup across the supply chain will be a key focus for future observation.
Note: This is not the first time the MLCC industry has experienced such a shortage cycle. In June 2017, Yageo issued a second wave of price adjustment notices for certain product lines: lead times extended from the original 1.5 months to up to 6 months, with prices raised 15% to 30%, and some items seeing even higher increases. Certain specific part numbers (such as Murata's NPO capacitors supplied for Apple wireless chargers) even experienced nearly 10-fold price surges at one point. This shortage wave only began to ease by mid-2018, after which prices fell significantly as end-market pull-in cooled and distributors shifted to inventory digestion, completing a full shortage—premium bidding—destocking cycle.
From the supply side, although major manufacturers have begun expanding production, new capacity will only come online progressively quarter by quarter, making it difficult to quickly close the supply-demand gap in the near term. Against the backdrop of exponentially growing demand for high-capacitance, high-reliability MLCCs driven by AI servers, leading manufacturers that control capacity and long-term client agreements will continue to hold pricing power, while small and medium-sized clients will face the dual pressures of rising costs and unstable supply for at least the next year.
