### Tanker rates, already higher than most imagined possible, just spiked again :: Lloyd's List

(https://www.lloydslist.com/LL1158620/Tanker-rates-already-higher-than-most-imagined-possible-just-spiked-again)

Tanker rates, already higher than most imagined possible, just spiked again
    - US-Europe suezmax index jumped 150% week on week to $577,792 per day; West Africa-Europe suezmax index rose 145% w/w to $567,138 per day
- West Africa-China VLCC index is at $697,160 per day, up 43% w/w; US Gulf-China VLCC index is at $473,958 per day, up 20% w/w
- US Gulf-Europe aframax index is at $313,794 per day, up 44% w/w; Caribbean-US index is at $335,288 per day, up 70% w/w
                
        
        
            
    
            Crude tanker markets hit a tipping point in mid-September, surging to a new level as more crude exited the Strait of Hormuz. Over the past three days, rates crossed another tipping point, spiking even higher due to the lagged global ‘cascade’ effect
JUST when you thought crude tanker rates couldn’t possibly go higher, they’re surging to even more extreme levels.
The “cascade” effect has gone into overdrive, with already historic very large crude carrier strength causing suezmax rates to spike, and suezmax demand pulling up aframax rates.
The tight supply of ballast VLCCs in the Atlantic has forced charterers to split stems into suezmax loads (VLCCs carry 2m barrels, suezmaxes 1m barrels, aframaxes 750,000 barrels).
The consequence: suezmax rates have gone parabolic over the past three days, mirroring the near-vertical trajectory of VLCC rates in mid-September.
The decision of Middle East Gulf producers to bring more tankers through the Strait of Hormuz under US military protection, even as Iran ratchets up attacks, has sharply increased cargo supply in the near term, attracting more tankers to wait for ship-to-ship transfers outside Hormuz.
VLCCs loading via STS in the Gulf of Oman are earning around 50% more than VLCCs loading in the Atlantic basin. The ballast leg from Asia to the GOO is also much shorter than to the Atlantic.
Clarksons Securities puts the current wait time for a GOO STS at seven to 10 days, and estimates that STS volumes have surged from 5m-6m barrels per day a month ago to 12m bpd currently.
This combination of factors is soaking up more VLCC tonnage, creating massive knock-on effects that have caused spot rates to shoot upwards in the suezmax and aframax segments.
“Even in a crazy market, shipowners tend to make rational decisions,” wrote Erik Broekhuizen, head of tanker research at Poten & Partners, in a report on Saturday. “The earnings discrepancy has kept VLCCs closer to the Asian market, leaving it to suezmaxes and aframaxes to do the heavy lifting out of the US Gulf, turbocharging their earnings.”
“Atlantic freight is repricing violently on real tightness,” said Sparta Commodities. “The Atlantic-MEG divergence is the defining feature.”
Clarksons said in a report on Monday: “Given the lucrative earnings potential in the Gulf of Oman, vessel availability in other markets has become notably thinner, leading to a scramble for any available tonnage. The rapid increase in Middle East crude flows has put the squeeze on all tanker classes.”
Fearnley Securities said: “Available tonnage is seemingly vacuumed off position lists the second it is marketed.”
Suezmax spot indexes
Suezmax owners have been the big winners in the recent surge. Atlantic basin suezmax rates have more than doubled in the past three days — and they were already at or near all-time highs even before the latest jump.
The Baltic Exchange’s West Africa-Europe suezmax time charter equivalent index was at $567,138 per day on Monday, up 145% week on week.
Guyana-Europe was at $584,012 per day, up 150% w/w. US Gulf-Europe was at $577,792 per day, up 150% w/w.
The Black Sea-Mediterranean suezmax index — which has an added premium due to the Russia-Ukraine war — was at a staggering $801,077 per day on Monday, up 115% w/w.
All the Baltic Exchange’s suezmax indexes hit new all-time highs on Monday.
VLCC spot indexes
Lower VLCC availability as a result of the logjam in the Middle East initially brought suezmax rates up due to stem splitting, but in recent days, one could argue that suezmax strength is supporting VLCC rates, particularly in the Atlantic basin.
Suezmaxes have half the crude cargo capacity as VLCCs, and if suezmax rates are more than double VLCC rates, which they are, VLCC owners may hold out for more.
The Baltic Exchange’s West Africa-China VLCC index was at $697,160 per day on Monday, up 43% w/w. The US Gulf-China VLCC index was at $473,958 per day, up 20% w/w. Both are at all-time highs.
The Oman-China VLCC index has been steadier. It was at an astronomical $860,580 per day on Monday, up 5% w/w and just below the all-time high reached on September 17.
The world’s most famous VLCC index, the Baltic Exchange’s TD3C, is perhaps the least relevant to current earnings across the broader owner market. It measures a direct voyage from Saudi Arabia to China, whereas the vast majority of MEG crude is shuttled to STS positions in a two-part process.
That said, the TD3C could be viewed as a proxy for the total freight. It hit yet another all-time high on Monday, at $1.33m per day, up 10% w/w.
Aframax spot indexes
Atlantic aframax rates fared historically well at the very beginning of the Hormuz crisis. During the initial panic to replace MEG crude, Asian buyers booked aframax loads from the US Gulf; these unusually long-haul voyages took capacity out of the market and caused Atlantic aframax rates to skyrocket.
Those early peaks were not revisited — until now. The cascade effect on aframax rates in the last few days has rewritten the record books.
The Baltic’s US Gulf-Europe aframax index was at $313,794 per day on Monday, up 44% w/w; the cross-Med index was at $343,227 per day, up 19% w/w, Caribbean-US was at $335,288 per day, up 70% w/w; and North Sea-Europe was at $348,651 per day, up 10% w/w.
“Increased suezmax activity continues to provide further support for aframax demand,” said Clarksons in a brokerage report on Monday, referring to the US Gulf-Europe aframax trade.
How high can crude tanker rates go?
There is a psychological anchor in shipping at certain rate levels: a TCE of $100,000 per day has traditionally been the bellwether of VLCC upcycles.
Tanker shipping’s “Overton window” — the window of thinkable possibilities — now has an extra zero, at $1m per day.
“Our imagination is obviously not imaginative enough, because we’ve been lagging the reality by far,” said Jorgen Lian, shipping analyst at DNB Carnegie, during last week’s Capital Link conference in New York.
How high can spot rates go? The rate can be whatever charterers are willing to pay for spot, with the caveat that charterers will, as financial pain increases, belatedly expand period coverage and reduce spot exposure.
The freight ceiling in the commercial equation is set by the crack spread: the refining margin. If importers have to pay too much in freight to earn a profit selling products refined from the transported crude, they generally won’t transport the crude.
The crack spread is historically high amid the Hormuz crisis, allowing crude tanker freight to rise much more than was previously imagined possible.
Also, there is an energy security factor, which may prompt state-controlled buyers to temporarily overpay for freight to secure volumes now, particularly if they are concerned that US protection of shuttle tankers is not indefinitely guaranteed, and/or that there will be military escalation within the coming months.
Broekhuizen noted that VLCC spot rates were around 3% of the delivered cost of the crude cargo at the beginning of this year, pre-crisis, and the freight share has now shot up to 27%.
This freight percentage of delivered cost may be unprecedented for crude shipping but it is not particularly exceptional in dry bulk shipping.
Breakwave Advisors pointed out that Atlantic-to-China freight rates comprised over 40% of the landed cost for iron ore last week. Brazil-China capesize rates were around $40 per tonne at the time, very strong but far from historic highs.
Commenting on the current dynamic for crude tankers, Clarksons Securities said: “As capacity gets scarcer, the balance shifts from owners competing for cargoes to charterers competing for ships. This is why refining margins matter so much.
“Once the supply curve is close to vertical, the question increasingly becomes how much the marginal cargo can afford to pay.”
Clarksons said that for every $10 per barrel gain in crude freight, VLCC TCE increases by around $400,000 per day, excluding commissions and assuming unchanged voyage costs.
It cited Argus estimates that the Singapore refinery product mix could sell for $151 per barrel last week, implying a pre-freight margin of around $40 per barrel, while Oman-Asia VLCC freight was around $21 per barrel, meaning “there is still considerable margin left for VLCC owners to capture”.
If VLCC owners were to capture the entire refining margin, which Clarksons said is “unlikely in practice”, Oman-China VLCC rates would be almost double current levels, at $1.6m per day.
“The reverse is also important,” Clarksons cautioned. “Rates do not need more ships to become available before they fall. If refinery margins weaken, charterers’ willingness to pay can fall sharply even while vessel availability remains tight.”
