### Chinese lithium giant says price swings have turned more extreme

(https://m.miningweekly.com/article/chinese-lithium-giant-says-price-swings-have-turned-more-extreme-2026-11-11)

Chinese lithium giant says price swings have turned more extreme
Lithium prices are getting harder to predict after a rollercoaster ride this year, according to Ganfeng Lithium Group, one of China’s top producers of the battery metal.
Market swings have become more extreme and price cycles have gotten shorter, president assistant Roy Zhang said at a conference in Shanghai on Wednesday.
“We have seen an entire price cycle just from the beginning of this year, which is not quite common,” said Zhang. “I don’t think anyone has an obvious view about where the price will be in the next 6 to 12 months.”
Lithium remains a nascent, fast-growing market, which makes it challenging to forecast as supply, demand and trading activity all expand at a breakneck pace. On the Guangzhou Futures Exchange where the metal is traded, sentiment rather than market fundamentals often dominates activity.
The market has been engulfed by heightened volatility this year due to uncertainty over supply from major production hubs, and changes in the structure of demand. In addition to electric vehicles, large batteries for energy storage systems have emerged as a meaningful pillar of consumption and are playing a bigger role in assessing the industry’s outlook.
Spot prices of lithium carbonate in China are 23% lower this year at 91 500 yuan ($13 700) a ton, after slumping in recent weeks on growing concerns over the demand outlook. They had rallied to over 200 000 yuan in May.
Zhang said lithium demand is growing at 40% this year and supply is catching up quickly, which is “quite phenomenal.” But the market’s balance is delicate.
“Even a small disruption in the supply chain will change the market very fast,” he said. “Just one substantial failure of a mine operation could very quickly shift the market into a shortage.”
