### Iran reportedly seizes UAE-owned tanker in Hormuz as Gulf shipping tensions escalate · Business Upturn

(https://businessupturn.com/asia/middle-east-asia/iran/iran-reportedly-seizes-uae-owned-tanker-in-hormuz-as-gulf-shipping-tensions-escalate-6/)

Iran has reportedly seized a UAE-owned tanker in the Strait of Hormuz, Iranian state news agency Fars reported, adding a potentially significant new escalation to a maritime crisis that has already sharply reduced commercial traffic through the strategic waterway. The report did not immediately provide the tanker’s name, flag, cargo or the circumstances surrounding the alleged seizure.
The claim comes as tensions between Iran and the United Arab Emirates have intensified over shipping through Hormuz. Independent confirmation of the reported seizure was not immediately available from the UAE government, the vessel's operator or maritime security authorities in the sources reviewed. The United Kingdom Maritime Trade Operations agency, which monitors shipping incidents in the region, maintains a live incident reporting system for vessels operating around the waterway.
A new escalation in an already disrupted waterway
The reported seizure comes only days after the UAE accused Iran of attacking vessels operated by Abu Dhabi National Oil Company (ADNOC) while they were transiting the Strait of Hormuz.
The UAE said on Aug. 14 that one of its ADNOC vessels had come under attack while navigating the waterway and called on Tehran to halt what it described as unprovoked attacks and reopen the strait. No injuries were reported in that incident.
The Associated Press also reported that two ADNOC-operated oil tankers were attacked by drones while transiting Hormuz on Aug. 14. The UAE blamed Iran for the attacks, while the incidents added to concerns about the security of commercial shipping.
Iran has not publicly acknowledged the specific seizure reported by Fars in the material currently available.
Why Hormuz matters
The Strait of Hormuz connects the Persian Gulf with the Gulf of Oman and the Arabian Sea and is one of the world's most important energy corridors.
Under normal conditions, roughly one-fifth of global oil and LNG supplies pass through the waterway. Recent fighting and restrictions, however, have drastically reduced shipping activity. Reuters reported that traffic through Hormuz had fallen by about 97% from historical levels amid the conflict.
The slowdown has already affected energy markets, shipping costs and the movement of crude, LNG and other commodities.
A confirmed seizure of another UAE-linked tanker could therefore have consequences beyond the vessel itself, particularly if shipping companies respond by avoiding the waterway altogether.
Iran's control claims add to uncertainty
The reported seizure also comes as Tehran and Washington remain locked in a dispute over who controls maritime movement through Hormuz.
Iranian officials have repeatedly said vessels require Iranian authorisation to pass through the strait, while the United States has challenged Tehran's claims and sought to maintain maritime access. Reuters reported last week that Iran and the United States were making competing claims over control of the waterway.
Iranian officials have also warned that Tehran could shift toward a more offensive military posture if diplomacy fails, increasing concerns over further maritime confrontations.
What happens next?
The immediate focus will be on identifying the tanker, establishing its flag and ownership, and determining whether Iranian authorities officially acknowledge the seizure.
The UAE and international maritime-security agencies are also expected to assess the report and establish whether the vessel and its crew are safe.
For global energy markets, the key question is whether the reported seizure remains an isolated incident or becomes part of a broader pattern of vessel interceptions.
With commercial traffic already severely disrupted, another confirmed tanker seizure would add to pressure on governments and shipping companies to find safer routes through or around one of the world's most critical maritime chokepoints.
