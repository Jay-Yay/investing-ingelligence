ADNOC suffers sixteenth hit as Houthis strike Saudi refinery
Iran attacked another ADNOC vessel in the Strait of Hormuz over the weekend, with the Houthis simultaneously striking Saudi Arabia’s energy infrastructure.
The UAE said an ADNOC-owned vessel was hit by an Iranian missile early on Saturday while transiting Hormuz. No casualties were reported and ADNOC said the situation was brought under control.
The UAE foreign ministry described the strike as an act of piracy and called on Tehran to stop targeting commercial shipping and reopen the strait fully.
ADNOC revealed on Friday that 15 of its vessels had been attacked by missiles and drones since the US and Israel went to war with Iran in February. One seafarer has been killed and 20 injured.
The latest strike came as negotiations over restoring traffic through Hormuz remained deadlocked, with Iran demanding sanctions relief, compensation for war damage and the withdrawal of US forces from the region before fully reopening the waterway.
Further west, the Houthis claimed a drone attack on Saudi Aramco’s 400,000 barrel-per-day Jazan refinery on Sunday.
Saudi Arabia’s energy ministry confirmed that a fire broke out at the Red Sea facility but was extinguished without injuries. It did not state what caused the blaze.
The Houthis also launched missile and drone attacks against the Yemeni port of Mocha, close to the Bab el-Mandeb, killing seven people and causing severe damage to port infrastructure, according to Yemen’s internationally recognised government.
The Saudi refinery strike came after the Houthis declared a naval blockade against the kingdom last month and stepped up attacks on Saudi-linked tankers and Red Sea infrastructure.
Meanwhile, the financial consequences of the Hormuz conflict have become clearer for Thailand’s Precious Shipping.
The company received $10.9m from war-risk underwriters following the loss of the 30,193 dwt Mayuree Naree (pictured), which was hit by two projectiles while passing through Hormuz on March 11, one of the most high profile shipping casualties in the US-Iran war to date .
The attack caused a major engine-room fire. Three crewmembers died and the 2008-built bulker subsequently grounded off Iran’s Qeshm Island before being declared a constructive total loss.
Precious Shipping said the payout, received in April, primarily covered the loss of the vessel and compensation for crewmembers and their families.
