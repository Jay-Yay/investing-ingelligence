### Petrobras' Deepwater Push Sets Off a Race for Offshore Vessels | OilPrice.com

(https://oilprice.com/Energy/Energy-General/Petrobras-Deepwater-Push-Sets-Off-a-Race-for-Offshore-Vessels.html)

Recently, we reported that the Middle East conflict and subsequent closure of the Strait of Hormuz has triggered an unprecedented spike in freight and shipping rates, with Very Large Crude Carrier (VLCC) spot rates now exceeding $1 million per day. The sharp spike is now reshaping oil flows and forcing producers to cut prices in a bid to help buyers offset extra transport costs. The situation is a little different in Brazil, where the offshore sector is facing a potential vessel shortage as demand outruns supply amid the offshore oil boom, positioning the region as the global offshore support vessel (OSV) market's primary growth hub. Driven by soaring production from Petrobras (NYSE:PBR) at deep-water assets such as the Buzios field, the gap between required tonnage and available fleets is rapidly widening.
Arctic Offshore senior market analyst Martin Lysne expects Brazil to need close to 40 more vessels by 2030 to support its scaling operations.
"The question is not whether Brazil needs more vessels," Lysne said. "It is where they will come from."
Tidewater (NYSE:TDW) president and CEO Quintin Kneen sees high-specification Platform Supply Vessel (PSV) day rates reaching US$55,000-60,000 within five years. Brazilian law reserves offshore support work for Brazilian-flagged vessels, and foreign vessels can be chartered in only when no local vessel is available. However, the local fleet cannot keep pace with demand, opening a lucrative window for international operators.

To stimulate local construction, Brazil's oil and gas regulator (ANP) has backed new incentives that already support around 36 OSVs and 13 tankers across Brazilian shipyards, according to ANP local content manager Gustavo Tinoco. According to Kneen, a new PSV needs about US$44,000 a day for 20 years just to break even, meaning that owners can only commit the required capital if they are secured through long-term contracts.
Tidewater has answered Lysne's question by buying Brazilian tonnage instead of building it. In August, the Texas-based operator completed its acquisition of Wilson Sons Ultratug for an aggregate $500 million, including roughly $283.1 million paid in cash at closing and the assumption of around $229.3 million in existing debt. The deal added 22 PSVs and expanded Tidewater's Brazilian fleet to 28 OSVs. Nineteen of the acquired vessels are Brazilian-built and share a common design, which simplifies crew training and maintenance. The transaction lifted Tidewater's global fleet to 231 vessels, including 213 OSVs, and added roughly $441 million in contract backlog.
Thankfully, Brazil's vessel manufacturers and operators have some distinctive advantages. Brazil is the world's second-largest ethanol producer and third-largest biodiesel producer after the United States and Indonesia, according to the International Renewable Energy Agency, turning out an estimated 9.7 billion liters of biodiesel in 2024, about three-quarters of it from soybeans. In 2024, Petrobras began selling bunker fuel blended with 24% biodiesel to ships off the Brazilian coast, Brazil's first renewable marine fuel.
The country's shipyards are also turning out hybrid platform supply vessels (PSVs). These hybrid configurations combine conventional fuel-operated engines with alternative power sources to reduce carbon emissions, cut fuel consumption, and lower maintenance needs. Two years ago, Petrobras awarded $2.8 billion in contracts for 12 hybrid platform supply vessels to modernize its offshore logistics fleet and support exploration and production. The modernization project features a 40% local content construction requirement, injecting billions into Brazilian shipbuilding and creating roughly 11,000 jobs. Built by Detroit Brasil for Starnav Serviços Marítimos, the Starnav Elektra was recently delivered as Brazil's first domestically designed and built battery-hybrid PSV. It features a 1,322 kWh lithium iron phosphate battery system from WEG and operates under a long-term charter to Petrobras.
SEACOR Marine Holdings (NYSE:SMHI), another Texas-based operator, already runs hybrid-powered PSVs in the region. The company operates a diverse fleet that includes platform supply vessels, fast supply vessels and hybrid-powered PSVs. Through a joint venture involving UP Offshore, it manages and deploys Brazilian-flagged offshore support vessels such as PSVs and ROV support vessels. SEACOR continues to service multi-year contracts for hybrid-powered PSVs in Brazil, as outlined in its First Quarter 2026 Results and Nasdaq press release.
Additionally, Petrobras' subsea goods and services manager Carolina de Carvalho says that deploying uncrewed surface vessels (USVs) promises up to 90% lower fuel use compared to traditional manned vessels, while standard subsea tree concepts could cut capital needs by up to 30% through standardized designs that streamline manufacturing and simplify installation.
The investment angle is clear here for long-term investors. Shipping has been one of this year's best trades. The Breakwave Tanker Shipping ETF (BWET), which tracks crude tanker freight futures, gained 5,265% in the 12 months to October 19, making it the best-performing exchange-traded fund in the U.S. this year. Offshore support vessel owners with Brazilian exposure have posted smaller but steadier gains. Tidewater shares are up 67.7% over the past 52 weeks, giving the company a market value of about $4.0 billion, while SEACOR Marine is up 48.8%, with a market cap of about $236 million.
