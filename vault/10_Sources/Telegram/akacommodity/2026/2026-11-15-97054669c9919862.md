### BNEF forecasts 209 GW of new batteries in 2027 - pv magazine USA

(https://pv-magazine-usa.com/2026/11/13/bnef-forecasts-209-gw-of-new-batteries-in-2027/)

BNEF forecasts 209 GW of new batteries in 2027
ccording to the latest BloombergNEF forecasts, new global energy storage installations will reach 188 GW/535 GWh in 2026, a 45% increase in capacity compared to 2025. China, the United States, and Australia will account for 62% of the new deployment.
In China, the market is maintaining a high rate of installation despite the end of a mandate that linked storage to new renewable projects. New support mechanisms include a firm capacity requirement for certain stand-alone solar and wind projects and a target of 300 GW of storage by 2030.
The United States continues to grow, supported by tax incentives and demand for data centers. In India, tender awards will push new installations above 6 GW in 2026, up from a few hundred megawatts the previous year.
In Europe, in addition to the United Kingdom, Germany and Italy, markets such as Bulgaria, Romania, Poland, France, Greece, the Netherlands and Ukraine will reach gigawatt installation scales.
By 2027, BNEF forecasts 209 GW/638 GWh of new installations, an 11% increase in capacity. Markets accelerating their deployment include Spain, the United Arab Emirates, Chile, and Japan. By 2036, cumulative global capacity could reach 3 TW/11 TWh, roughly ten times the 2025 levels.
Storage associated with photovoltaics
Hybridization with renewables, especially photovoltaics, represents a growing part of the deployment. By the end of 2025, there were 69 GW/196 GWh of storage associated with 299 GW of solar capacity.
Energy storage is being incorporated into renewable energy projects in response to increased generation curtailment and price cannibalization in markets such as the United States, Australia, and Chile. In addition to China, other countries have introduced regulatory or auction-linked requirements for renewable energy projects with storage, including India, Mexico, and the Philippines.
Regionalization of supply chains
Localization policies are reshaping the supply chains for batteries and energy storage systems. The United States is combining tariffs and incentives for domestic manufacturing with restrictions on equipment and components linked to Chinese supply chains.
Security of supply and cybersecurity are also shaping the policies of the European Union and Japan. As a result, Chinese equipment manufacturers are increasing their activity in markets with fewer restrictions, especially the Middle East, Latin America, and sub-Saharan Africa. South Korea, India, and Brazil have also adopted industrial protection measures, although less stringent than those in the United States.
LFP and sodium-ion
Lithium iron phosphate (LFP) will continue to be the primary chemical used in storage over the next decade, due to its cost competitiveness, cycle life, and supply chain availability.
The rising cost of materials associated with LFP is driving interest in sodium-ion batteries. This technology offers a lower risk of fire and a wider thermal operating range, which can reduce the need for liquid cooling and the energy consumption of auxiliary systems.
Its adoption remains limited by smaller industrial scale and its economic competitiveness compared to LFP. These limitations also affect other long-life storage technologies not based on lithium.
