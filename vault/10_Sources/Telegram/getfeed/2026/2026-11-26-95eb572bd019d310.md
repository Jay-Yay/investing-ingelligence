As we’re entering the peak holiday season, forecasts once again point to robust consumer spending, continuing a broader trend of remarkable resilience. Throughout recent years, Americans have kept the economic engine humming by continuing to open their wallets despite stubborn inflation, higher borrowing costs and elevated economic uncertainty. Yet beneath that resilience lies a consumer base that has become far more calculated and cost-conscious.According to survey data from Statista Consumer Insights, nearly half of Americans report having adjusted their spending habits over the past 12 months in response to the rising cost of living. Among those who shifted their behavior, outright cutbacks and bargain hunting lead the way: 40 percent stated they are spending less overall, while 39 percent said they are comparing prices more frequently before making a purchase.Consumers are also actively trimming their shopping baskets, with roughly a third buying fewer items overall or cutting back on non-essential purchases. Meanwhile, value-seeking tactics have become mainstream – 31 percent report switching to cheaper alternatives more often and 30 percent actively hunt for discounts.While shoppers are unlikely to make major sacrifices during the holiday season, these findings suggest that retail success this winter will largely depend on who can offer the best deals, clear price transparency and compelling value to an increasingly cost-conscious public.This chart shows how Americans changed their spending habits in 2026.
