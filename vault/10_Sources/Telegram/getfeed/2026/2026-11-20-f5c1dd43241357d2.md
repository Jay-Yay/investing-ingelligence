Global electricity generation could increase by 78 percent by 2050, according to the latest edition of the International Energy Agency’s World Energy Outlook. Of the 57,800 terawatt-hours produced by that time, nearly two-thirds will come from renewable energy sources, according to the forecast. The IEA projects that the amount of electricity generated from these sources will rise from 10,380 to 37,919 terawatt-hours – an increase of 250 percent. Alongside this well-known trend, another one is emerging. The so-called nuclear renaissance is also expected to make gains, albeit on a much smaller level. The amount of electricity generated by nuclear power is projected to more than double by 2050, accounting for approximately 11 percent of global electricity production. For comparison, the current figure is around 9 percent. Meanwhile, the role of fossil fuels is set to decline, as shown in the Statista chart. Nevertheless, even by the middle of the century, gas, coal, and oil will still account for nearly a quarter of total electricity production, while carbon capture in connection with fossil fuel burning will still only play a very small role.The countries most responsible for this increase are India, expected to almost tripe its electricity demand until the middle of the century to reach 9.5 percent of the global total, as well as China, where energy demand is expected to rise by 77 percent to 34 percent of worldwide demand. Responsible for 12 percent of the world's electricity demand in 2050, the U.S. will grow it's energy hunger by 42 percent.
