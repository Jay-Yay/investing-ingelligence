### Money in Your Pockets: Governor Hochul Signs Bill Restricting Exploitative Vehicle Subscription Fees | Governor Kathy Hochul | New York State

(https://www.governor.ny.gov/news/money-your-pockets-governor-hochul-signs-bill-restricting-exploitative-vehicle-subscription)

S.9348A/A.10411A Prohibits Car Companies from Charging for Built-In Features
Amid Rising Consumer Fees, New Law Increases Price Transparency and Puts Money Back in New Yorkers’ Pockets
Governor Kathy Hochul today signed a bill restricting motor vehicle manufacturers and dealers from charging consumers subscription fees after they purchase a vehicle. Amid rising consumer prices, the new law guards against a rising trend of car companies charging car owners fees to activate and use built-in features like heated seats, enhanced navigation, hands-free driving and entertainment options that do not present an ongoing expense for dealers and manufacturers.
“New Yorkers are already struggling with rising prices due to Trump’s affordability crisis, and we’re not going to let harmful business practices increase the cost of living even more,” Governor Hochul said. “People spending their hard-earned money on a car shouldn’t have to fork over even more money for features that should automatically come with the car. My message to car companies is clear: this kind of deceptive business model isn’t going to fly in New York State. Keep your hands out of New Yorkers’ wallets.”
S.9348A/A.10411A prohibits motor vehicle manufacturers, dealers or third-party service providers from adding subscription services or charging post-purchase fees for any motor vehicle feature that uses pre-installed components or hardware that do not have an ongoing cost. As vehicles become more advanced, the new law aims to establish clear rules preventing exploitative practices and prevent post-purchase fees as a future automotive business model.
New York Attorney General Letitia James said, “No one should have to pay recurring fees just to use features in a car they already bought. My office has secured millions of dollars for New Yorkers throughout the state who were charged illegal junk fees when purchasing a car. Unfair fees are fueling rising costs, and I am proud to work with Governor Hochul and the legislature to bring costs down.”
State Senator James Skoufis said, “People ought to know they’re able to use their already-installed remote start or heated seats during the winter without a monthly subscription. This new law will simply and clearly prohibit car manufacturers from ripping off New Yorkers who already paid for vehicle features when they purchased their car. It’s a common sense law and I’m grateful for the Governor and Assemblymember Magnarelli for their partnership enacting this legislation."
Assemblymember William B. Magnarelli said, "I thank Governor Hochul for recognizing the importance of this legislation, particularly as technology continues to evolve at a rapid pace. This law will help protect New Yorkers from unfair fees and ensure consumers aren’t paying extra for features already built into their vehicles.”
The law will take effect in 90 days on February 27, 2027.
This new law is the latest in Governor Hochul’s ongoing commitment to help address the cost of living by putting money back in the pockets of New Yorkers statewide. Under Governor Hochul’s leadership, she has:
- Secured reforms to lower auto insurance premiums for New Yorkers, battling fraud, limiting damages paid out to bad actors, and ensuring consumers, not insurance companies, are prioritized.
- Signed legislation requiring businesses to notify consumers of upcoming renewals and price changes as well as provide clear instructions on how to cancel subscriptions.
- Cut middle-class taxes to their lowest rates in 70 years.
- Tripled the Child Tax Credit, providing families up to $1,000 per child under the age of four and up to $500 for school-aged children, beginning in 2026.
- Made school meals free for every student, saving families $1,600 per child every year.
- Sent Inflation Refund checks to 8.2 million New Yorkers — up to $200 for individuals and up to $400 for families.
- Increased the maximum weekly unemployment benefit by $300 a week.
