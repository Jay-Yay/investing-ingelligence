In recent years, the United States has witnessed a significant increase in the adoption of smart home devices, marking a major shift in the way Americans interact with their living spaces. From intelligent thermostats keeping your living room nice and cozy and automated lighting systems setting the scene to voice-activated assistants switching on your coffee machine first thing in the morning, connected devices have become integral components of modern households. Apart from the inevitable realization that hitting a switch is actually a pretty convenient way of turning on the lights, the potential convenience and efficiency gains offered by well set-up smart home devices cannot be denied. But how smart are American homes more than a decade after Amazon introduced the Amazon Echo and Alexa, giving the smart home its voice? And what kind of smart devices do Americans use? According to data from Statista Consumer Insights, home security is a key driver of smart home adoption, with 37 percent of respondents saying they own a connected camera systems, smoke alarms or other devices made to improve building safety. Apart from security, convenience is a major factor, with smart appliances and connected lighting/electricity solutions also far up on the list.
