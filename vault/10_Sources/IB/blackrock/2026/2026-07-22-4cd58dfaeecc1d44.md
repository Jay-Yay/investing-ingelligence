Key takeaways
• Strong demand continued to absorb record issuance, 
supporting municipal market performance.
• Summer reinvestment flows should provide a supportive 
technical backdrop, although elevated issuance may 
temper the seasonal tailwind.
• Municipal credit fundamentals remain broadly healthy, 
although rich relative valuations support a more 
measured approach to adding risk.
Quarterly review
Municipal bonds were among the best-performing fixed 
income sectors in the second quarter, as robust demand for 
tax-exempt income drove strong total return. Unlike many 
taxable fixed income sectors, where returns were driven 
primarily by coupon income, municipal investors also 
benefited from meaningful price appreciation as tax-exempt 
yields declined across the curve. The Bloomberg Municipal 
Bond Index ultimately returned 2.50% for the quarter, 
generating 234 basis points of excess return versus 
Treasuries.
Elevated issuance remained a defining theme throughout 
the quarter. June supply totaled approximately $62 billion, 
representing the largest monthly issuance since October 
2024 and the third-highest monthly total on record. Even 
so, the market absorbed that volume with little disruption. 
Strong fund flows, balanced across open-end mutual funds 
and ETFs, and continued demand for tax-exempt income 
helped support market performance despite elevated 
supply. Midyear municipal fund flows totaled $61.5 billion, 
already matching the full-year total for 2025 and ranking 
among the strongest first-half totals on record.
Market outlook
Seasonal technicals should  support the municipal market 
through much of the third quarter. July and August have 
historically been favorable months for performance as 
reinvestment income from maturities, calls, and coupon 
payments exceeds new issuance. We expect approximately 
$98 billion of reinvestment proceeds during the summer 
months, providing an important source of demand as 
investors put cash back to work.
This year's seasonal backdrop, however, may be less 
supportive than in prior years. Following record issuance in 
2025, municipal borrowing has remained elevated, with 
year-to-date issuance running approximately 5% ahead of 
the same period last year.  As a result, we expect net 
issuance to decelerate but remain modestly positive, rather 
than turn meaningfully negative as it has in recent years. 
This suggests the seasonal tailwind is likely to be more 
modest this summer, which leaves less room for investment 
error at today’s rich relative valuations. 
Municipal-to-Treasury ratios ended the second quarter 
below their one-, three-, and five-year averages across much 
of the curve. Although we do not view rich relative valuations 
as a bearish signal, they support a more measured approach 
to adding risk. We prefer to look for concessions in the 
primary market and expect periods of market weakness to 
create more attractive entry points, remaining methodical 
and opportunistic when deploying capital.

Muni spotlight: Strong fundamentals, increasing credit differentiation
Municipal credit fundamentals remain broadly healthy 
entering fiscal 2027. State and local governments continue 
to benefit from strong balance sheets built during the post-
pandemic recovery, providing many issuers with meaningful 
financial flexibility even as revenue growth normalizes. 
Combined state reserve balances are projected to total 
approximately $274 billion, equal to roughly 20% of general 
fund spending, while Pew Charitable Trusts estimates rainy-
day fund balances remain approximately 65% above pre-
pandemic levels. State and local government debt has 
declined as a share of U.S. GDP, reflecting years of 
disciplined fiscal management. Collectively, these metrics 
suggest the municipal market remains on solid financial 
footing.
The credit issue for fiscal 2027 is not broad fiscal weakness, 
but the durability of budget balance as revenue growth 
normalizes. Governors' proposed budgets point to slower
revenue growth following the extraordinary gains of recent 
years, while recurring spending pressures build across 
education, Medicaid, pensions, and infrastructure. Property 
tax reform proposals in several states add another layer of 
uncertainty where replacement revenue sources remain 
unclear.
For portfolio positioning, we continue to favor issuers with 
recurring budget balance, preserved reserves, manageable 
fixed costs, and limited reliance on one-time resources. 
Conversely, issuers relying heavily on fund balances, narrow 
revenue measures, or optimistic federal cost assumptions 
warrant closer monitoring. Credit selection will be critical in 
healthcare and higher education, where fundamental 
divergence across issuers remains significant. By contrast,
 we remain constructive on housing, transportation, and 
other revenue-backed sectors supported by resilient 
underlying fundamentals.

Investment involves risk. The two main risks related to fixed income investing are interest rate risk and credit risk. Typically, 
when interest rates rise, there is a corresponding decline in the market value of bonds. Credit risk refers to the possibility that 
the issuer of the bond will not be able to make principal and interest payments. There may be less information available on 
the financial condition of issuers of municipal securities than for public corporations. The market for municipal bonds may be 
less liquid than for taxable bonds. A portion of the income from tax-exempt bonds may be taxable. Some investors may be 
subject to Alternative Minimum Tax (AMT). Capital gains distributions, if any, are taxable. Index performance is shown for

We enter the third quarter maintaining a neutral duration posture.  Absolute yields remain attractive, but we are using 
valuations as our guide as we move forward with investment. 
We favor the 20-year segment of the municipal curve, where investors can capture approximately 90% of the curve's yield 
while benefiting from attractive roll opportunities. Across portfolios, we also maintain an emphasis on higher-quality 
structures and higher-coupon bonds that we believe are better positioned to navigate periods of elevated interest 
rate volatility.
Sector View Rationale
Revenue-backed Overweight We favor revenue-backed credits over general obligations as political uncertainty, property 
tax debates, and fiscal pressures increase the need for stable, dedicated revenue streams.
Housing Overweight Affordable housing, LIHTC bonds, and high-grade state housing finance agency programs 
provide attractive yield and enjoy durable bipartisan policy support.
Transportation & 
essential services Overweight Essential-service issuers continue to benefit from resilient demand and stable cash flows.
AI-related 
infrastructure Selective Financings tied to AI data center development, including prepaid energy transactions, offer 
potential to enhance yield via exposure to strong technology-related credits.
Healthcare Selective We like organizations with strong balance sheets, stable operating performance, and 
favorable long-term demographic positioning.
Higher education Selective We prefer issuers with resilient enrollment trends and stronger financial profiles.
Puerto Rico Monitoring Greater clarity around the Financial Oversight and Management Board could improve the 
outlook for Puerto Rico credits.
