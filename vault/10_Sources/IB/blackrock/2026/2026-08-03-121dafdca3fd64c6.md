French bonds: a lesson in selectivity 
• France’s government bond sell-off shows why the drivers of higher yields 
matter. We favor select opportunities across sovereign bonds and credit. 
• Tech stocks were under pressure last week over scrutiny on the record pace of 
the AI buildout. We still favor AI within our neutral stance on U.S. equities.                                               
• Jittery euro-area bond markets will be watching Moody’s decision on France’s 
credit rating closely. We also eye UK CPI data for a read on global inflation.                                               
Higher bond yields are creating new investment opportunities, as reflected in our 
move to upgrade U.S. Treasuries last week. Yet the sharp sell-off in French 
government bonds is a reminder that intensifying competition for capital is putting 
borrowers under greater scrutiny, making it increasingly important to understand 
what is driving yields higher and whether investors are adequately compensated 
for the risks. Selectivity is key in this environment. We favor select opportunities 
across sovereign bonds and credit.

The French bond sell-off illustrates how competition for capital is reshaping fixed 
income markets amid a global rate reset. Rising government borrowing and AI-
related investment are pushing up financing costs, exposing borrowers with 
deteriorating fundamentals to greater scrutiny. At the same time, higher yields 
bring more opportunities: more than 80% of global bonds now yield above 4%, 
versus 6.3% five years ago. See the chart. France shows why selectivity matters 
across fixed income though. Its 10-year yield has jumped about 100 basis points 
since early August to nearly 5%, reflecting the broader rate reset. Yet the 
widening spread over German Bunds shows investors are also demanding 
additional compensation for France's fiscal and political risks. This underscores 
why investors need to distinguish between the forces driving yields higher.

Spillover risks from France warrant close attention. But today’s starting point is materially different from that of the 2011-12 
euro zone debt crisis. France’s government debt has surged from roughly 89% of GDP in 2011 to around 119% today, while 
the euro area’s aggregate debt ratio is now back at its 2011 level of around 89%. The region is also better equipped to 
weather financial stress, with stronger banks and more powerful European Central Bank tools, though intervention isn’t 
guaranteed. Further volatility and spillovers remain possible. But indiscriminate selling could create opportunities in markets 
such as Spain, where fundamentals may not justify higher yields. For now, we stay neutral on euro-area government bonds 
and prefer opportunities elsewhere. 
We see opportunities where higher sovereign bond yields better compensate investors for risk without signaling weaker 
fundamentals. We upgraded U.S. Treasuries as higher starting yields provide a bigger cushion against further rate increases. 
We also see scope for markets to unwind excessive Fed tightening expectations. Japan offers a similar example of shifting 
value. Long-term government bond yields have climbed above 3% and the term premium has normalized, improving the 
risk-reward balance as much of the yield adjustment we anticipated has now occurred. That prompted us to trim our 
underweight, even as we expect further upward pressure on yields as monetary policy normalizes. We favor emerging market 
local-currency debt, particularly in Latin America, where improving fundamentals and finances support attractive yields. 
Across fixed income, we see a rise in term premium, particularly if driven by fiscal concerns, as a key risk that could push 
yields higher. 
The same selectivity extends to corporate bonds. We favor a reverse barbell approach, seeking attractive income in higher-
rated high-yield bonds while avoiding risks at either end of the credit spectrum. Heavy AI-related issuance is putting pressure 
on investment-grade bonds, while the weakest borrowers face greater refinancing challenges as rates stay elevated. Higher 
yields mean investors no longer need to reach as far down the credit spectrum for income.
Bottom line: France’s government bond selloff comes amid intensifying competition for capital that’s prompting investors to 
take a closer look at the source of higher yields. We favor select sovereign bonds and credit.

Market backdrop 
Concerns over surging AI-driven capital spending weighed on tech stocks last week. The S&P fell 2% while the Nasdaq 
slumped nearly 5%. U.S. 10-year Treasury yields briefly touched 5.34% after a hot September CPI print before retreating 
during the tech sell-off, though they remained up on the week. These moves reflect two key questions hanging over markets: 
the outlook for interest rates and the pace of the AI buildout. We still favor AI within our neutral stance on U.S. equities.

Equities
Developed markets
United States We are neutral. Strong AI fundamentals remain supportive, but policy uncertainty could fuel 
near-term volatility. We continue to favor AI and tech.
Europe We are neutral. We would need to see more business-friendly policy and deeper capital markets 
for Europe to outperform. We favor financials, infrastructure and industrials.
UK We are neutral. Valuations remain attractive relative to the U.S., but we see few near-term 
catalysts to trigger a shift.
Japan We are neutral. Strong corporate balance sheets and governance reforms remain supportive. 
We prefer targeted exposures to physical AI and the buildout’s bottlenecks. 
Emerging markets We are overweight. Strong earnings and cheaper valuations create opportunities. We 
particularly like different expressions of the AI scarcity theme across Asia and Latin America.
China We are neutral. We see opportunities in physical AI. Cheap, open-source AI could drive adoption, 
but that doesn’t necessarily translate into AI-provider profitability.
Short U.S. Treasuries
We are overweight. Much of the repricing we anticipated has occurred, and expectations for 
further Fed hikes look excessive. We favor the front end for attractive income and a greater 
breakeven buffer.
Long U.S. Treasuries We are neutral. Higher yields offer attractive income and a bigger cushion against further rate 
increases. We still see room for yields to rise as competition for capital intensifies.
Fixed Income
Global inflation-linked 
bonds
We are neutral. We see inflation settling above pre-pandemic levels, but markets may not price 
this in the near term as economic growth could slow.
Euro area govt bonds
We are neutral on short- and medium-term bonds. Current ECB rate pricing looks fairly valued 
given the balance of risks. Higher energy prices could push rates higher. We prefer to deploy 
risk elsewhere. 
UK gilts We are neutral. We expect periods of elevated volatility given political uncertainty, longer-term 
bonds making up a larger market share, and buyers becoming more price-sensitive.
Japanese govt bonds We are underweight. The substantial repricing in yields has improved the risk-reward, but 
further rate hikes and heavy issuance could push yields higher.
China govt bonds We are neutral. China bonds offer stability and diversification but developed market yields are 
higher. A shift in investor sentiment toward equities limits upside. 
U.S. agency MBS We are neutral and lean into the stable income MBS provide despite tight spreads.
Short-term IG credit We are neutral. Spreads are tight due to corporate strength, but attractive overall yields help 
offset the risk of increased issuance.
Long-term IG credit We are underweight. We prefer short-term bonds less exposed to interest rate risk over long-
term bonds. But attractive overall yields help offset the risk of increased issuance.
Global high yield We are neutral. High yield offers attractive income. We prefer higher-rated U.S. and European 
high yield over investment grade and see dispersion of returns increasing. 
Asia credit We are neutral. Overall yields are attractive and fundamentals are solid, but spreads are tight. 
Emerging hard currency We are neutral. Fundamentals have improved, but we see a more attractive risk-reward profile in 
EM local debt.
Emerging local currency We are overweight. We like the yield relative to its volatility and improving fundamentals.
