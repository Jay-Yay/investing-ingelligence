### Anthropic builds in-house chip design team, signaling AI firms' turn to custom silicon

(https://vo.la/5pCZcvD)

Anthropic is building an in-house team to design custom chips for its Claude AI models, according to Reuters, moving ahead with a plan first reported in April as the startup responds to a shortage of processors needed to run and develop more advanced AI systems.
The company is hiring engineers with experience across the hardware and software stack to co-design custom chips and AI models. Anthropic said the approach could make Claude run faster and more efficiently at the scale required by customers.
Anthropic describes custom silicon as the latest step in its multi-chip strategy and plans to continue relying on a diversified hardware stack that includes technology from Amazon Web Services, Google, Nvidia and AMD.
The company did not disclose a development timeline or say whether it intends to manufacture the chips itself.
Designing an advanced AI chip can cost roughly US$500 million, industry sources estimate, reflecting the cost of hiring specialized engineers and investing heavily to ensure advanced designs can be manufactured without defects.
Claude training expands into chip design
Separately, Anthropic is seeking to improve Claude's ability to assist with semiconductor design.
A job posting for a research engineer within Anthropic's reinforcement-learning organization describes a role focused on advancing the company's AI models' ability to design silicon.
The engineer would develop reinforcement-learning environments and evaluations for tasks including agentic generation of register-transfer-level code, design verification and physical-design optimization.
Register-transfer level, or RTL, describes how a chip's digital logic operates. Verification checks whether a design behaves as intended, while formal verification uses mathematical methods to determine whether it satisfies specified properties. Physical-design optimization covers the placement and routing of circuits to meet performance, power and area targets.
The position sits within Anthropic's Code RL team and seeks candidates with experience in ASIC or FPGA development, electronic design automation tools, and taking chips from specifications through tape-out and into silicon.
The posting does not identify the role as part of Anthropic's custom-chip team or say Claude will be used to develop the company's own processors.
Samsung talks and the OpenAI comparison
TechCrunch, citing The Information, reported in July that Anthropic had been in contact with Samsung Electronics to explore a potential collaboration involving the planned chip. At the time, Anthropic had not decided what the chip would be used for, how it would fit into a server or how powerful it would be.
Anthropic was also quoted as saying that chips from Google, Amazon and Nvidia would remain central to its diversified compute strategy. The company declined to comment further on a potential Samsung partnership.
Anthropic's move comes as AI developers pursue custom silicon to tailor hardware to specific workloads and reduce their dependence on Nvidia, the dominant supplier of AI accelerators.
In June, OpenAI unveiled Jalapeño, a custom inference accelerator developed with Broadcom, saying the chip went from initial design to manufacturing tape-out in nine months. Inference refers to running a trained AI model to generate responses or perform other production workloads, while tape-out is the stage at which a chip design is finalized and prepared for fabrication.
OpenAI's models were also used to accelerate parts of Jalapeño's design and optimization process. The project offers a comparison with Anthropic's separate effort to train Claude on silicon-design tasks, although Anthropic has not said Claude is involved in its custom-chip program.

### Tesla taps Samsung Electro-Mechanics, LG Innotek for Cybercab camera modules, report says

(https://vo.la/Crr4qjd)

Tesla has reportedly selected Samsung Electro-Mechanics and LG Innotek to supply camera modules for the Cybercab, a move that would give the South Korean suppliers a foothold in the automaker's purpose-built robotaxi as both expand beyond smartphones into automotive and physical AI applications.
South Korean business daily Hankyung reported on August 4 that the two companies had secured the entire initial camera-module order, with Samsung Electro-Mechanics starting mass production in July and LG Innotek expected to begin production by the end of 2026.
The reported initial production run is estimated at 20,000 to 30,000 Cybercab vehicles. Hankyung did not disclose the contract value or allocation between the two suppliers.
Tesla requested modules using 5-megapixel complementary metal-oxide-semiconductor (CMOS) image sensors, compared with the 1.2-megapixel sensors used in older Hardware 3 vehicles, according to the report. Camera modules capture visual data used by autonomous-driving software to identify roads, other vehicles, pedestrians, and traffic signals.
Reports differ on LG Innotek's order status
A separate EBN report offered a more cautious account, saying Samsung Electro-Mechanics was believed to be supplying Cybercab camera modules but describing LG Innotek as still discussing a possible arrangement with Tesla.
Both companies declined to confirm customer-specific information. Samsung Electro-Mechanics said it had discussed camera-module opportunities for robotaxis and humanoid robots during its second-quarter earnings call but could not identify a customer or confirm a supply agreement. LG Innotek also declined to comment on customer-related matters.
The conflicting accounts leave LG Innotek's order status unconfirmed. Hankyung said the company had already secured business and would begin production by year-end, while EBN said negotiations were continuing.
Samsung Electro-Mechanics' official earnings materials nevertheless show that it is expanding its automotive camera operations. The company said second-quarter automotive camera revenue grew as it supplied specialized products to major Korean and overseas customers.
The supplier also reported increased supply of high-resolution sensing and all-weather cameras to a global electric-vehicle manufacturer. Its third-quarter plans include supplying a specialty product for a global EV customer's next-generation platform and conducting pilot mass production of a high-resolution sensing module for humanoid robots. Samsung Electro-Mechanics did not identify the customers.
Camera suppliers move beyond smartphones
Samsung Electro-Mechanics and LG Innotek remain major smartphone camera-module suppliers, but both are stepping up efforts in automotive sensing, robotics, and physical AI as they seek additional sources of growth.
LG Innotek announced a partnership with Japan's TDK in July to develop next-generation vision sensing modules that integrate cameras with inertial sensors, position sensors, and microphones. The companies also plan to develop tactile sensing modules that allow robots to detect contact and pressure.
LG Innotek expects to introduce its first inertial-sensor-equipped vision module in 2027. It is also developing composite sensing systems that combine cameras, lidar and radar and has expanded cooperation with technology and robotics companies, including Boston Dynamics.
Tesla confirmed in its second-quarter update that Cybercab production had begun at Gigafactory Texas. The automaker described the vehicle as the intended workhorse of its Robotaxi fleet and said engineering test drives of production Cybercabs had begun on public roads. It also began offering rides to employees at its Texas campus in July.
The company listed installed annual manufacturing capacity for the Cybercab at more than 125,000 vehicles. Tesla cautioned that installed capacity does not represent the current production rate, which can be affected by component availability, equipment uptime, factory upgrades, and regulatory considerations.
If confirmed, the reported business would give Samsung Electro-Mechanics and LG Innotek an opportunity to apply their smartphone camera expertise to a production robotaxi platform. Tesla's official materials, however, do not identify either company as a Cybercab supplier.
