### [News] NVIDIA Vera Rubin Spillover from HBM to NAND: CMX Fuels TLC Spot Price Rebound from June Dip

(https://vo.la/8Ba93Pj)

[News] NVIDIA Vera Rubin Spillover from HBM to NAND: CMX Fuels TLC Spot Price Rebound from June Dip
As NVIDIA’s Vera Rubin ramps up, its memory impact is extending beyond HBM into NAND. Alpha Economy reports that stronger Rubin production is tightening TLC NAND supply-demand, with the 512Gb TLC NAND spot price rebounding from its June dip to $21.125 on Aug. 17.
Citing DRAMeXchange, the report suggests the price jumped 4.97% from the previous week and 11.6% in just three weeks.
CMX: A New NAND Demand Driver
Economy Tribune points to NVIDIA’s rollout of Context Memory eXtension (CMX) in the Vera Rubin platform as a key catalyst behind the TLC NAND price surge. As generative AI and AI agents handle longer contexts, KV caches storing previous-token computation results are expanding rapidly, making it costly and capacity-intensive to keep them entirely in expensive HBM.
Built on BlueField-4, CMX addresses this bottleneck by inserting a flash-based memory layer between HBM and network storage, allowing KV caches and other data pushed out of HBM to be stored in flash and retrieved when needed. Wccftech highlights that CMX uses TLC flash connected to Rubin GPU clusters via BlueField-4 DPUs and Spectrum-X Ethernet to handle KV cache in real time.
The scale of CMX could translate into a significant new NAND pull. Citing industry observers, Economy Tribune estimates that a single CMX configuration could use around 576 enterprise SSDs, delivering a massive 9,600TB (9.6PB) of total storage capacity.
While much of NVIDIA’s TLC NAND supply is likely secured through long-term contracts, Wccftech notes that the sheer scale of Rubin-related demand is already putting pressure on the spot market, raising the question of how far the price rally could go.
Samsung, SK hynix and Kioxia in Focus
As Economy Tribune notes, major NAND players including Samsung, SK hynix and Kioxia stand to benefit. Kioxia unveiled its CM10 enterprise SSDs in July, built on 10th-generation BiCS FLASHTM TLC NAND and optimized for AI inference and context caching.
Designed to support NVIDIA’s CMXTM architecture, the CM10 delivers higher performance, better power efficiency and greater cooling flexibility than the CM9, Kioxia said, adding that samples are now shipping to select customers.
SK hynix, meanwhile, is doubling down on high-capacity QLC enterprise SSDs through its subsidiary Solidigm. The company began mass-producing the world’s first 321-layer 2Tb QLC NAND in August last year and started shipping 321-layer QLC-based SSDs in April, Economy Tribune reports.
On the other hand, Economy Tribune notes that Samsung, in its semi-annual report on Aug. 14, noted that both DRAM and NAND posted record-high bit shipments in Q2, while the share of bit shipments to servers also reached an all-time high.
In July, Samsung also began mass production of the PM1763 enterprise SSD for NVIDIA’s Vera Rubin platform. Built with 9th-generation V-NAND and a 4nm controller, the 16TB model delivers up to 28,400MB/s read and 21,900MB/s write speeds, roughly twice those of its predecessor, according to the Chosun Daily.

### Nvidia swaps credit support for exclusive chip sales in OpenAI's Ohio data center campus

(https://vo.la/WscXF7C)

Nvidia has reached a deal with OpenAI to provide up to US$105 billion in credit guarantees for the AI startup's upcoming 8GW data center campus in Ohio. The support will help OpenAI to secure a lease from SB Energy, while Nvidia will be the facility's exclusive chip supplier.
Nvidia's guarantee applies to the project's initial 4.25GW phase, and in return, it will be the sole supplier of chips for this first phase, although it can expand this commitment later to cover the entire campus, according to The Wall Street Journal. Nvidia's full DSX AI factory stack will be used at the site.
"We are securing long-lived infrastructure for Nvidia compute so OpenAI can deploy the most productive AI factories that can be upgraded repeatedly with each new generation delivering more intelligence and better economics," said Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang in a statement.
As part of this deal, Nvidia will invest US$1.5 billion in SB Energy, a SoftBank-backed data center site developer that is planning an IPO next month. Under the terms of the deal, SB Energy will build, own, and operate the data centers while leasing them out to OpenAI for 20 years. The first 800MW of capacity is expected to come online in 2028, mainly using existing grid infrastructure.
The campus in Pike County, Ohio, is one of the largest ever planned, and the companies involved offered a number of concessions to the local community. SB Energy and SoftBank agreed to invest US$4.2 billion in the regional grid, while OpenAI and SB Energy are each giving US$40 million for local priorities such as healthcare and schools. OpenAI said that the project will create 35,000 construction jobs through 2032, as well as 2,500 long-term roles.
Nvidia's credit support covers part of the lease and power costs. It is also a commitment by the chipmaker to ensure that the site retains a minimum value, even if OpenAI were to default on rent. If this were to happen, SB Energy would seek to re-lease the site, or sell if it does not find another tenant. Nvidia would then cover the difference – up to US$105 billion – between the agreed-upon minimum value and the amount that SB Energy can recover.
Land and power are becoming critical bottlenecks in infrastructure, so Nvidia's credit support appears to give OpenAI a stepping stone toward overcoming them. This could open up hundreds of billions of US dollars in chip sales from OpenAI, according to Reuters, though this has led to worries about circular financing.
Nvidia reportedly pared down its original plans to guarantee US$250 billion after its stock dropped due to investor worries about risk exposure. However, the Wall Street Journal reported that by backing up the asset value of the site rather than OpenAI's lease, Nvidia is limiting its risk exposure. Tech companies are increasingly turning to capital markets to fund their AI builtout, with Nvidia recently bringing together major financial institutions to raise US$500 billion for AI infrastructure investments.

### 앞서가는 TSMC...옹스트롬급에서 '후면 전력-기존 설계' 동시 달성 - 전자신문

(https://vo.la/a37Ch1Q)

TSMC가 옹스트롬급(2나노 이하) 최첨단 선단 공정인 A16에서 후면 전력 공급 기술 우위를 확보하며 경쟁에서 한 발 앞섰다. 후면 전력 기술은 기존 칩에서 전력 공급 경로와 신호 경로가 동일 공간에 혼재하며 발생하던 병목 현상을 해결하기 위해 등장한 기술이다.
18일 업계에 따르면 TSMC는 업계 최초로 '슈퍼 파워 레일(SPR)' 후면 전력 공급 방식이 적용된 옹스트롬급 CMOS 플랫폼 A16을 성공적으로 개발·검증했다. 기존 N2P(N2 개량버전) 공정의 게이트 밀도와 설계 유연성(NanoFlex)을 그대로 유지한 것이 이번 성과의 핵심이다. 
기존 반도체 칩에서는 전력 배선(전기 공급)과 신호 배선이 모두 칩 앞면에 함께 배치된다. 그런데 공정이 미세화될수록 두 배선이 좁은 공간에서 서로 간섭하면서 배선 혼잡(병목) 심화와 전기 저항 증가로 인한 전압 강하(IR Drop) 문제가 발생한다. 
이를 해결하기 위해 후면 전력 기술을 적용하려면 트랜지스터 배치 방식이나 셀 구조를 상당 부분 변경해야 한다. 공정이 미세화될수록 이 문제는 심화되며, 전기를 칩 뒷면에서 공급하기 위해서는 기존 셀 라이브러리와 설계 노하우를 일부 포기해야 하는 경우가 일반적이었다.
TSMC는 전력 공급 경로를 칩 뒷면으로 완전히 분리하고, 전용 접촉(VB)을 통해 각 트랜지스터의 소스와 드레인에 직접 전력을 연결하는 방식을 적용했다. 이 과정에서 앞면의 게이트 구조와 셀 크기, 레이아웃 면적을 거의 변경하지 않아 기존 설계와의 호환성을 유지할 수 있었다.
성능 향상 폭도 뚜렷하다. N2P 대비 동일 전력에서 속도를 8~10% 높이거나, 동일 속도에서 전력을 15~20% 줄일 수 있다. 칩 밀도 역시 8~10% 높아진다. 복잡한 신호 경로와 고밀도 전력망이 필수인 AI·고성능 컴퓨팅(HPC) 칩에 특히 적합한 것으로 평가된다. A16 공정의 양산은 올해 4분기부터 시작될 예정이다.
후면전력공급을 먼저 상용화한 인텔의 경우 '파워 비아'는 테스트 단계에서 핀 수 조정과 금속 피치 완화 등 기존 셀 구성을 변경하고 셀 아키텍처를 재설계한 바 있다. 삼성전자 역시 SF2 공정에서 후면 전력 도입을 추진 중인 것으로 알려졌으나, 인텔과 유사한 방향으로 진행될 가능성이 높다.
업계 관계자는 “해당 성과는 AI 가속기처럼 전력과 성능을 동시에 확보해야 하는 제품에서 설계 부담을 줄이면서 효율을 높일 수 있는 기술”이라며 “옹스트롬급 시대에는 공정 기술과 설계 생태계 양면에서 기술 경쟁이 치열해지고 있다”고 설명했다
